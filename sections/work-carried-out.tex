For the first time, this ambitious "experiment" brought together three major European communities involved in nuclear physics and accelerator/detector technologies for high-energy physics — each engaged in curiosity-driven research and its technological and societal applications.

\tsubsubsection{WP3 - Access to RI for Accelerators}

WP3 is connected to the provision of TA to RIs related primarily to High Energy Accelerator R\&D. The WP groups fourteen (14) facilities located in eleven (11) research laboratories, spread out across Europe. The facilities are grouped into four Tasks, targeted to specific areas of R\&D: material testing, technology infrastructures, electron and laser beams, and applications. 

During PR3, the main focus was on optimising the use of project resources to maximise Transnational Access (TA) delivery and on completing and implementing the service improvements outlined in the MS19 report.

To address the uneven demand across WP3 facilities, funds were progressively reallocated to those receiving more user requests, while preserving opportunities for facilities with limited activity to accommodate new users until late in the reporting period. This flexible approach helped increase the number of Access Units (AUs) delivered despite the constraints faced by some facilities.

In parallel, all planned service improvements were successfully completed, with most already put into operation for the benefit of users.

The User Selection Panels (USP) per Task established in P1, involving external experts at 50\% level,  continued with only a recent change, and operated successfully throughout the reference period as required.

\tsubsubsection{WP4 - Access to RI for Detectors}
\end{prtwo}


WP4 served to provide TA to top level European RIs for R\&D on detectors. Transnational access to RIs is well tailored, in terms of freely available resources at facilities, to the needs of detector R\&D where dedicated funding is often a problem. The slate of RIs is chosen to match the needs of European (and wider) detector R\&D carried out in the framework of DRD collaborations, set up by the ECFA Detector R\&D Roadmap, and monitored by DRDC at CERN. The access is spread over three types of research infrastructures, grouped into tasks: test beams (3 RIs), detector characterization (2 RIs), and irradiations (6 RIs). Service Improvements (Task 4) were executed at each RI to improve user access.

The key objective of WP4 in P3 was to continue running the TA framework for executing user projects of high relevance to the detector R\&D community, and to improve performance at RIs with TA deficit in the first two periods. The overall success was excellent, but still not entirely uniform across the RIs. The 124 additional projects, executed in P3, augmented the user count by close to 500 to more than 1000 users in total. The total access unit delivery amounted to 165 \% of the initial pledge. The USP common to all WP4 RIs served well; TA project applications were accepted with a high success rate due to careful pre-evaluation by the Facility Coordinators. With the commitment of the RIs that spent most of their access allocation in P1\&P2, to provide additional resources, the RIs of WP4 were continuing to facilitate Detector R\&D during the entire four years of the EURO-LABS project. The schedule delay of HL-LHC provided an unforeseen bonus, having CERN-based facilities available for an additional year. Consorted measures were been taken to bring the few under-performing RIs to their expected performance with remarkable success for most of them.
