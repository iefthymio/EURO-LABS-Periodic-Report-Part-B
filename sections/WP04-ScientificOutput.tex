\subparagraph{Work Package 4 - Access to RIs for Detectors} \mbox{}


WP4 is the enabler of Detector R\&D, the fundament of current and future scientific endeavour. Most of its scientific output is thus hidden, displaced to the developed detectors, their implementation in experiments and the fundamental physics discoveries those experiments shall unravel. Needless to list what shall be discovered – if we knew that, there would be no need for conducting experiments.



WP4 is of vital importance for the successful completion of the HL-LHC project, the focal point of the European Strategy for Particle Physics (ESPP) in 2013,its update in 2020 and the recently adopted update in May 2026. The upgrades have long finished their R\&D, the construction of the detectors is in full swing and large detector assemblies are starting to appear. There were, however, late surprises (like the bPOL problem),that still required verification of the solutions in the WP4 RI’s. The experiment upgrades for the LHC are amply documented in the respective Technical Design Reports (TDR) and in the Memoranda of Understanding (MoU) for the specific detectors, available on the CERN Document Server (CDS).


The focus in WP4 was synchronized with the stipulations of the ECFA Detector R\&D Roadmap, having served either the medium term goals like the Higgs factory or the more distant endeavour towards the highest energy hadron collider (FCC-hh). WP4 backed up the newly set up DRD collaborations by providing them with access to top-level infrastructure needed for their detector R\&D.  


The spectrum of DRD collaboration research activities is best illustrated in their proposals and their structuring into Work Packages and Working Groups. They follow closely the programme, set up by  the DRD Themes (DRDT) specified in the Detector R\&D Roadmap.

Given the strong community preference, propagating to the 2026 ESPP Update, for FCC-ee as the next flagship project at CERN, the future focus in R\&D infrastructure will emphasize test beam facilities, with irradiations limited to relatively low fluences and SEE tests.



WP4 was set up to deliver the infrastructure needed for detector R\&D. The resulting scientific output in terms of publications is two-fold: scientific results in instrumentation have been published in scientific journals and conference proceedings during the duration of the project. The more glorious part with the physics results of the HL-LHC experiments and their follow-ups at future colliders will, however, start emerging during the next decade and beyond, when the detectors tested in WP4 RIs in the coarse of EURO-LABS will be put to their proper usage and deliver precious physics data.
