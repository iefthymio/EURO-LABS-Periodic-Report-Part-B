\subsubsection*{Progress per Task}
\subparagraph{Task 2.1: Stable Ion Beams} \mbox{}


%\todo{Briefly explain the progress of the task in context to the DoA.}


Period 3 supported many experiments using  Stable Ion Beam facilities of Task 2.1 as in P1 and P2. All eight facilities (including consortia) have supported multiple experiments with a very high level of success. In total 225 projects were supported in P3.



The supported activities have enabled a very broad range of experiments to be carried out, focusing not only on forefront studies of the structure of atomic nuclei but also on use of ion beams for applications to benefit society and for the associated technology  development to be exploited in the fundamental studies. Significant examples of synergies exits, wherein development work at a smaller facility contributed to experiments at the larger facilities hosting world-class instrumentation. These examples serve to emphasize the importance of facilities of all scales in the EURO-LABS ecosystem.


At \textbf{GANIL} 12 stable ion beam projects, ranging from nuclear physics to interdisciplinary research, were carried out in P3, supported by ca. 1800 hours of beam time. 

At \textbf{ALTO} facility in IJCLab Orsay 1 long stable ion beam experiment, devoted to neutrino-less double beta decay, was supported during the P3 with 216 hours of beam time access. 

At \textbf{GSI/FAIR}, the NUSTAR collaboration carried out 15 stable ion experiments during P3, exploiting ca. 1700 Access Units.  The supported projects were covering a wide range of research topics in nuclear structure, nuclear astrophysics, and fundamental interactions

At the \textbf{IFIN-HH} 25 experimental groups were supported in P3 and received 5867 hours of beam time. The supported projects were covering such topics, as nuclear lifetime measurements, investigations of collectivity and shape coexistence, electromagnetic moments, or reaction cross-sections. Data analysis is ongoing for most groups, but several have already published or submitted their results for publication. 


At \textbf{JYFL} 11 stable ion beam experiments were supported during the reporting period. The experiments were granted more than 2200 hours of beam time access. The supported projects aimed on study, among others, g-factor measurements of states have a picosecond lifetimes, shape evolution and coexistence, delayed fission and muti-nucleon transfer.



At \textbf{INFN-LNL} 12 experiments were supported during P3,  using 3348 access units. The experiments exploited the European AGATA gamma-ray tracking array in various configurations with ancillary detectors. Using AGATA-SPIDER, a number of Coulomb excitation experiments were performed to study several aspects of nuclear structure (multiple shape coexistence, evolution of E2 collectivity, gamma decay of the narrow near threshold resonances, isospin mixing).
 Other devices combined with AGATA for experiments were AGATA-SAURON, AGATA-EUCLIDES and AGATA-OSCAR (see Fig.~\ref{fig:WP2_AGATA}). This shows the importance of the AGATA array and the versatility of the devices which can be modified for campaigns of experiments using different complementary techniques.

The performed projects included such topics as ion beam analysis of biological tissues (CLEAR-USE, Seville), development of antibacterial titanium dioxide nanotubes via ion implantation of Cu and Ag (USE-IST, Lisbon), study of alpha-particle induced reactions to constrain the weak r-process network calculations (CLEAR-ATOMKI, Debrecen).

\subparagraph{Task 2.4: Theoretical Support} \mbox{}

%\todo{Briefly explain the progress of the task in context to the DoA.}

P3 saw the continuation of the transnational (TA) and virtual (VA) access to theory tools and methods for nuclear experimentalists, organized in two subtasks.

WP 2.4.1 enables in-person \textbf{TA} to \textbf{ECT*} (the European Centre for Theoretical Studies in Nuclear Physics and Related Areas, Trento). During P3, ECT* hosted 35 workshops and 3 Doctoral Training Programs (DTPs), following calls for proposals in the spring and summer of 2024 and 2025. Calls were made through the ECT* website (\url{https://www.ectstar.eu}) and the extensive mailing list of ECT* associates. In addition, the ECT* Director and the Scientific Board actively solicited proposals to ensure a balanced annual program. Among the successful proposals, the Scientific Board, which serves as Selection Panel, assigned 17 EURO-LABS projects for P3: 15 workshops and 2 DTPs. These activities encompassed nuclear structure and reactions (often based on modern {\it ab initio} theory), nuclear astrophysics (for example, neutron-induced reactions), search for new physics with nuclei (such as neutrinoless double-beta decay and the X17 anomaly), and analysis methods of interest to both experimentalists and theorists (for example, machine learning and statistics). Highlights were the two DTPs, which provided training to 80+ students on two diverse topics, superconducting technologies for quantum sensing and computing, and nuclear and particle theory for new physics searches. The selected projects attracted 1122 Users, of which 122 were supported by EURO-LABS for a total of 684 Access Units (days spent by supported users at ECT*). 

WP 2.4.2 provides \textbf{VA} to theoretical tools through the infrastructure Theo4Exp, which has been available since 1 February 2024 at \url{https://institucional.us.es/theo4exp}. The main page provides links to three installations: MeanField4Exp at \textbf{IFJ PAN} (Nuclear Physics Institute, Krakow), Reaction4Exp at \textbf{USE} (University of Sevilla), and Structure4Exp at \textbf{UMIL} (University of Milano). An International Review Panel met every year to review and validate the progress made on the VA infrastructure and its three installations. A highlight of the reporting period was a hands-on workshop at ECT* in July 2025, which introduced the Theo4Exp infrastructure to about 35 researchers, most young experimentalists and most supported by EURO-LABS TA funds. In addition to training on using the VA interface, the workshop provided valuable feedback to the infrastructure developers. Since then, the three installations have been finalized. Throughout P3 the infrastructure continued to provide VA to the community through the application \url{https://iam-eurolabs.ijclab.in2p3.fr/login}, which has been developed by WP5 of the present EURO-LABS project. This application ensures access to any researcher affiliated with a scientific institution via either eduGAIN (\url{https://edugain.org/}) or ORCID (\url{https://orcid.org/}). 

In P3, 15 projects (virtual services) were offered and realized by 357 remote Users; 3953 Access Units (counted as each started hour of active use of the virtual service) were provided. The total number of Users (533) and Access Units (5869) during the duration of the project far exceeded estimates when EURO-LABS was proposed.

More details on the progress in this task is available in the report "Access to the Theory for Experiments facilities" - \url{https://web.infn.it/EURO-LABS/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/EURO-LABS_Deliverable_WP2-D2-4.pdf}.

WP2.5.1 Streamlined and remote access:

One of the main objectives of the EURO-LABS project was to facilitate access to European research infrastructures by providing researchers with clear, comprehensive, and up-to-date information about available experimental facilities and TransNational Access (TNA) opportunities.
To achieve this objective, it has been essential not only to develop the website but also to ensure its continuous maintenance, regularly update its content, and expand its structure to accommodate new information and services.
The EURO-LABS website (\url{https://www.slcj.uw.edu.pl/en/tna-euro-labs/}) serves as a central information platform for researchers interested in accessing experimental facilities participating in the project.
The homepage provides quick and convenient access to several key resources:
i) Current Calls for Proposals – information about available experimental opportunities, application procedures, and submission deadlines; 
ii) EURO-LABS Newsletter – the latest project developments, scientific activities, news, and announcements; 
iii) EURO-LABS Basic Training Schools – information about training opportunities and educational activities aimed primarily at students and early-career researchers;
iv) TNA Facility Pages – direct links to dedicated pages presenting individual research infrastructures participating in the project.

The dedicated pages of the participating accelerator facilities provide more detailed information, including: general descriptions and technical characteristics of the facilities; available experimental infrastructure, equipment, and particle beams; additional information and resources provided by the individual facilities.
The website structure and content have been progressively updated during P3 to improve navigation and ensure that relevant information is easily accessible to prospective users.

The website has been designed to simplify the process of identifying suitable experimental facilities and applying for access.
By presenting relevant information in a consistent and well-organised format, it enables researchers to compare experimental opportunities, identify appropriate research infrastructures, and find the procedures required to submit proposals.
Direct links between the homepage, current calls for proposals, and individual facility pages make it easier and faster for users to locate essential information.
The website also enhances the visibility of participating research infrastructures and their experimental capabilities within the wider European scientific community, thereby supporting international collaboration and more effective use of available facilities.

The EURO-LABS website has become a common information platform supporting TransNational Access to European research infrastructures. Its continuously updated content and improved structure facilitate access to information about experimental opportunities, participating facilities, and application procedures.
The website will continue to be maintained and updated after the completion of the EURO-LABS project. Particular attention will be given to publishing new calls for proposals and providing information about upgrades and developments in the experimental equipment available at presented facilities.
TransNational Access activities will be partially continued under the HADRON-2030 project, which officially started on 1 October 2026.
The website will soon be updated to include a link to the HADRON-2030 TransNational Access programme, once the detailed access procedures and conditions have been finalised.
In this way, the website will remain a valuable information resource for the European nuclear physics community, contributing to the long-term visibility and accessibility of research infrastructures and supporting continued international scientific collaboration

\begin{itemize}
\item A secure remote access system for users of the UMCG-PARTREC accelerator:  Recent geopolitical and health crises have highlighted the need to provide accelerator users with the means to perform experiments with minimal in situ personnel. At UMCG-PARTREC, a user-friendly system (see schematic shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:WP2_Remote1}) was developed to provide online access to irradiation controls (beam on, fluence, energy, etc) without compromising the IT system’s security. The system is composed of a Python server residing in the restricted accelerator network, which can receive user commands as text strings and interact with the pre-existing LabVIEW irradiation control system. Users do not have direct access to this network and can only communicate with the server over a serial connection. Due to its simplicity, the attack surface of said connection is minimal. Users are given a client device (optionally their own laptop) that connects to the serial interface and exchange text strings with the server. The client is connected to PARTREC’s unprivileged network and can be accessed via VPN. Camera feedback from the irradiation room and a non-interactive screen grab of the control system are available on the client. The remote access system is available on request. New features and a GUI will be added based on interest.

WP2.5.2 Targets for high intense beams: 

Subtask 2.5.2 of Work Package 2 focuses on developing targets for nuclear and applied physics through collaboration among different European research institutions. Its aim is to bring together experts in target manufacturing and characterization to improve the availability and diversity of targets for the research community. These targets support applications ranging from fundamental nuclear physics and neutron converters to medical radioisotope production. With the increasing availability of high-intensity accelerator beams, particular attention is given to developing high-quality, resistant targets that preserve their thickness, uniformity, isotopic composition and thermal properties under demanding irradiation conditions.

Specific activities in P3 of this subtask deal with the study of existing and novel materials, the improvement of current and development of novel fabrication techniques, the characterization procedures and the sharing of knowledge among the various facilities.
As an example, the new CACTUS (Chamber for Alpha-particle Characterisation of target Thickness and Uniformity by Scanning) setup (Fig.~\ref{fig:WP2_CACTUS_Targets} ) has been installed at LNS Catania. It has been used to characterize targets in terms of thickness and uniformity, by measuring the energy loss and spread of alpha particles from radioactive source passing through thin target layers. Such method has allowed to characterize and identify the best multi-layer graphene foils to be used as substrate for targets needed for experiments with high intensity beams.

WP2.5.3 Biomedical application (FLASH@EURO-LABS):

Significant progress within this sub-task culminated in the successful completion of three core research objectives: (i) the development and benchmarking of a numerical model tailored for Farmer-type ionization chambers; (ii) the subsequent application of this numerical model to evaluate the operational reliability of the TM31023 detector under carbon ion FLASH irradiation conditions; and (iii) the systematic improvement of dosimetry uncertainty frameworks across clinical facilities. The results obtained in the previous phase (see report MS14) were summarised in a scientific paper and submitted in a scientific paper on  Zeitschrift für Medizinische Physik - Journal of Medical Physics (impact factor 4.7). The manuscript titled "Dose-rate metrics for ionization-chamber recombination 1 in synchrotron-based ultra high-dose-rate proton and carbon-ion beams: application and validity of quasi-static response models” has been accepted for publication on  October 26, 2026.





WP2.5.4 Improving Ion Beam services in variety and stability (ERIBS): 

The ERIBS project had two main objectives to improve the operation and ion beam services for the EURO-LABS infrastructures: 1) improve the production and availability of metal ion beams and 2) improve the stability of ion beams. Several approaches were taken to ensure that the objectives of the project could be met. As was demonstrated by the sub-reports of this deliverable document, remarkable improvements were made, and the objectives of the project were met.

Practically all development work was almost completed during the previous reporting period. The final reporting period (P3) mainly focused on finalizing and disseminating the achieved results. This included, for example, the technology transfer for high temperature ovens, instability diagnostics, and q-1 dipole. The characterisation of the q-1 dipole has successfully been completed during the reporting period. The diagnostics met and even exceeded the initial objectives, enabling continuous monitoring of the q-1 beam without disturbing the ion beam transported further for the acceleration. Additionally, the kinetic stability of the plasma can be monitored at the same time using the same diagnostics.
Specifically: 

1) The solution for the issues of the previously abandoned JYFL foil oven were found which made it possible to use it for elements requiring high evaporation temperature. This extended the safe oven operation temperature at JYFL from around $1200^\circ$C (JYFL miniature oven) to $1800^\circ$C. In addition to this development the IPHC Strasbourg team has successfully developed and tested the inductively heated high-capacity oven. Performed tests showed that this oven will further extend the maximum temperature of the evaporation ovens available for the EURO-LABS laboratories.

2) The commissioning of the q-1 diagnostics were performed using oxygen beam. The charge state spectra were measured with both Faraday cups, i.e. by using the primary and q-1 faraday cups. In this measurement the location of the q-1 faraday cup was 65 mm from the beam line axis. As Fig.~\ref{fig:WP2_Oxygen_ERIBS} demonstrates, the resolving power of the q-1 diagnostics is at least as good as in the case of the primary faraday cup. Therefore, the objective regarding the resolving power was clearly met The q-1 faraday cup is smaller when compared to primary faraday cup which explains the lower ion beam intensity. The commissioning will be continued by studying and defining the correlation between the intensity behaviour of the primary and q-1 faraday cups. It can be concluded that this project has been a great success, enabling the online beam monitoring and opening new research opportunities. This diagnostic technology is now available to all EURO-LABS partners.



WP2.5.5 Optimal employment of travelling gamma detectors (INTRANS):

The INTRANS subtask organised a Germanium Detector School at the University of Liverpool from 7th – 11th April 2025. The school was aimed at postgraduate students, postdoctoral researchers and electronics engineers who were not experts in germanium detector systems. The school was a complementary activity to the Hands-on Workshop on Test, Operation and Repair of detectors held at Legnaro National Laboratory (LNL) in September 2024. The school had 25 registered participants who took part in 4 different practical activities which focused on detector assembly, detector repairs and problem solving, the measurement of detector performance and the use of multichannel data acquisition systems. Lecture material was delivered covering key relevant topics, , and an invited talk was also presented by Michael Ginsz from Mirion Technologies, who discussed specialist detector manufacturing.

INTRANS also sponsored a workshop held at the University of Valencia 27-28th March 2025 to discuss and develop physics cases for future decay spectroscopy campaign at the Rare Isotope Beam Factory at Riken in Japan. 

Following the success of the first edition organized in Januray 2024 in Orsay, the second edition of the INTRANS physics workshop was held 2-5th February 2026 at LNL, Italy. The workshop gathered 90 participants and was dedicated to the presentation of the latest results obtained at international gamma-ray facilities, of recent theoretical studies and to the discussion on the challenges of future research, experimental campaigns and technical developments.

Finally, INTRANS contributed financially to the maintenance operations of expensive Ge detectors, which are shared between EURO-LABS infrastructures. In particular, the Ge detector laboratories of GANIL (France), JYFL (Finland), HIL (Poland) and LNL (Italy) benefitted from the purchase of electronic consumables crucial for detector and preamplifier diagnostics and repair.



While all Service Improvements tasks successfully met their objectives in Month 36, fine-tuning activities continued until the project's closure. \subsubsection*{Main Results and Achievements}

Progress in the TA/VA offer in Tasks 1-4, as well as the main achievements in the Service Improvements task 2.5 in the P3 period are described above and in the chapter "Scientific output of the users at the facilities". As the main achievement, linking almost all of the WP2 tasks, was the organization in ECT* of the workshop "Theory Service for the Low Energy Nuclear Physics Community: A Hands-on Workshop". This workshop took place in ECT* and was organized by the coordinators of the Theo4Exp VA facility on 7-9 July 2025.
The Theo4Exp workshop was designed to bring together experimental users of the TA facilities and theoretical nuclear physicists around a common goal: making theoretical tools more accessible and useful to the experimental community. A particular focus was placed on three virtual platforms, namely MeanFieldExp, Reaction4Exp, and Structure4Exp, developed to support experimental work by offering practical calculations and insights. The workshop aimed not only to introduce these tools, but also to encourage open discussion, gather feedback, and strengthen collaboration between experiment and theory. The total number of participants was 44, and among them 22 Users funded by EURO-LABS TA.  Over the course of the workshop, participants got a clear and hands-on introduction to each of the three platforms. The sessions helped understand the tools, showing how they can be used, from planning measurements to interpreting data. The open discussions were particularly interesting, allowing users to share suggestions and raise questions in a constructive manner. Beyond the technical content, the workshop created a friendly space for discussions between theorists and experimentalists, highlighting just how much both sides can benefit from closer collaboration. The positive engagement suggests real potential for these tools to be integrated into day-to-day research and even into teaching at the Master's level.
