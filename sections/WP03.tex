Over the 18 months covered by P3, the WP3 TA facilities provided a significant amount of transnational access to users, delivering a total of \textbf{8,506 Access Units (AUs)} (typically hours of beam time), through \textbf{40 projects}. The distribution of AUs delivered by each facility is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:wpe-taunits}.

Highlights of the experimental results obtained by the TA projects at the WP3 facilities are presented in Section~\ref{sec:wp3_scientific_output}.

As in previous reporting periods, the level of TA delivery varied across WP3 facilities during P3. Some facilities maintained a high level of activity, with four exceeding their total planned AUs, partly thanks to the reallocation of funds. Others, however, were unable to provide access due to technical or operational constraints.

Early in PR3, measures were taken to address this imbalance and make better use of the available resources. Following discussions at the Task and WP levels, funds were redistributed, first between facilities within the same Task, then across WP3, and finally to other WPs in the project. This approach allowed unused resources to be redirected towards facilities with higher demand and the capacity to provide additional access.

As a result, several facilities were able to accommodate more user projects and deliver additional AUs, in some cases exceeding their original allocations. This helped maintain a high level of TA activity despite the difficulties faced by some facilities, while supporting important ongoing research in the accelerator field.

Beyond transnational access, good progress was also made on the service improvements planned for eight facilities. \textbf{The planned improvements were successfully completed}, in some cases benefiting from additional funds made available through the redistribution of resources. Most of these improvements have already been put into operation, enhancing the facilities' capabilities and providing direct benefits to users.

\subsubsection*{Main Results and Achievements}

HiRadMat continued to provide transnational access (TA) to several users to conduct experiments spanning advanced accelerator materials, thermomechanical shock studies, radiation damage benchmarking, detector validation, and frontier plasma astrophysics. 

The obtained results were achieved thanks to the uniqueness of the facility, combining SPS beam energy, pulse intensity, and dedicated experimental infrastructure available at HiRadMat. This led to a consistent high demand of the facility throughout the project, along with sustained engagement from the international user community.


\paragraph{Service improvements:}

EURO-LABS supported service improvement studies for HiRadMat, focused on enhancing the calibration of the SPS Beam Position Monitor (BPM) system (designated "ALPS") in both the ring and the extraction line toward the facility. The studies led to the development of a systematic approach to optimise neural network models for accurately determining transverse beam position from BPM electrode signals. Additionally, they quantified training uncertainties and assessed the robustness of different network configurations.
\begin{figure}[!htb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{graphics/ServiceImprovements.png}
    \includegraphics[width=0.48\linewidth]{graphics/ServiceImprovements (2).png}

Right: Mean maximum error values over multiple runs using tanh as activation function throughout the network. The performance of the networks was worse when using tanh.  (Courtesy: V. Stergiou and A. Boccardi, CERN).}
    \label{fig:wp3-hrm-si}

A key element to highlight was the evaluation of the robustness and reproducibility of the training procedure. Repeated training enabled a systematic assessment of training variability and comparison of different network architectures, leading to the identification of a preferred feedforward network configuration. Further optimisation of some training parameters will be performed, in order to complete the evaluation of feedforward networks for SPS BPM calibration.

The results represent an important milestone from demonstrating the feasibility of machine learning approach towards a robust and reproducible calibration method suitable for practical implementation in the BPM system of the SPS. In particular, the main contribution provides an improvement of stability and reproducibility of beam delivery across the SPS complex and its wider experimental programme.



\subparagraph{Task 3.2 : Technology Infrastructures} \mbox{}

This task includes six specialized facilities focused on accelerator component R\&D. The facilities remain in close contact with ongoing R\&D initiatives, both in Europe, such as the I.FAST project\footnote{\url{https://ifast-project.eu}}, and worldwide, such as PIP-II\footnote{\url{https://pip2.fnal.gov}}, where a demand for tests of magnets and superconducting cavities has arisen. 
Technical work or upgrades initiated by the facilities' management to support long-term service contracts with large projects have reduced the availability of certain infrastructures for individual TA projects eligible for EURO-LABS funding. This is particularly the case for INFN-LASA, where, thanks to the efforts of the WP and Task Leaders and of the local Technical Coordinator, at least one TA project could be carried out. By contrast, the INFN-THOR, CERN-XBOX and UU-FREIA facilities did not host any TA projects, as explained below.

In summary, during the 18 months of P3, a total of 569 AUs were delivered, mainly through remote access, bringing the cumulative total since the beginning of EURO-LABS to 1,645 AUs. This corresponds to 19\% of the planned AUs when considering only the operational facilities, or 17\% when considering all six facilities as initially foreseen in the Grant Agreement.

The WP and Task Leaders implemented the planned mitigation measures, reallocating the resources freed up by these facilities to other tasks in WP3 (in particular Tasks 3.1 and 3.3), with part of them transferred to WP2.

Details per facility are provided in the following paragraphs.

\subsubsection*{Main Results and Achievements}

\subparagraph{UU-FREIA} \mbox{}

After a strong start in P1, when it delivered 50\% of the AUs foreseen in the Grant Agreement, FREIA provided no transnational access in P2 or P3. Preliminary discussions on testing a PIP-II cavity did not lead to any follow-up.

The main obstacle was the limited availability of the facility for small, short-term TA projects, owing to its commitment to large, long-term contracts for the series testing of production modules for major future European accelerators. These tests heavily constrain the use of the liquefier, which is essential for short experimental campaigns. 

The resources not used by FREIA were reallocated within WP3 as part of the mitigation plan described above.

\subparagraph{INFN-LASA} \mbox{}

INFN-LASA (Laboratory for Accelerators and Applied Superconductivity) operates four test facilities, dedicated to superconducting magnets, superconducting RF cavities, high-brightness photocathodes for electron sources, and laser applications to high-power Fabry--Perot cavities and advanced timing systems, originally conceived to support the laboratory's internal research. Opening them to external users proved slower than planned: the limited staff were heavily involved in planning the expansion of the laboratory, which will double its surface area, while the upgrade of critical infrastructures (electrical systems and cooling systems for cavities and magnets) made the facilities unavailable to external users. Once these upgrades were completed, in the second part of 2025, several access requests were discussed, from DESY and CEA, JLab, IJCLab and Uppsala University, covering tests of SC RF cavities, laser systems and a high-voltage DC photocathode test stand, although their realisation was constrained by the limited remaining time and the availability of resources such as liquid helium.

In the end, LASA was able to host a single TA project in June 2026, shortly before the end of EURO-LABS. The project, carried out in collaboration between CEA-Saclay, Fermilab and LASA, aims to develop plasma processing for the PIP-II 650~MHz 5-cell low-beta (LB650) cavities. Plasma processing is an established technique for mitigating field emission and multipacting, which limit the accelerating gradient of SRF cavities. Building on earlier simulations of plasma ignition in this geometry, the project focused on the first experimental tests at LASA, using an LB650 cavity equipped with a cryomodule-style high-power coupler, a key step towards demonstrating the technique under realistic operating conditions.

The resources not used by INFN-LASA were reallocated within WP3 as part of the mitigation plan described above.

\subparagraph{INFN-THOR} \mbox{}

The INFN-THOR (Test in HORizontal) facility in Salerno is dedicated to testing superconducting magnet systems and is equipped with advanced cryogenic, vacuum and electrical test set-ups. Throughout the project, its activity has been driven by the high-priority support to GSI/FAIR: until June 2024, the facility performed the final acceptance tests of the SIS100 quadrupole doublet modules before their installation in the SIS100 tunnel in Darmstadt, and from July 2024 to February 2025 it was closed to external users for an upgrade and consolidation aimed at improving its efficiency and testing throughput.

In P3, INFN-THOR remained fully engaged in the series production of superconducting magnets for GSI/FAIR and could not host any TA project.

The resources not used by INFN-THOR were reallocated within WP3 as part of the mitigation plan described above.

\subparagraph{IJCLab/SUPRATECH} \mbox{}

No Transnational Access (TA) was offered during the reference period, as the facility was fully committed to upgrading its cryostat and preparing for testing activities associated with the PIP-II production phase (2026–2027), leaving no availability for external R\&D users.

The remaining funds were reallocated to other facilities.

\subparagraph{CEA-IRFU/Synergium} \mbox{}

The CEA-IRFU Synergium hosts two platforms specialised in material science at cryogenic temperatures: \textbf{MACHAFILM} and \textbf{CRYOMECH}. Its cryogenic laboratories are involved in several European accelerator projects on superconducting RF (SRF) and magnet technology, with strong collaborative ties to major institutes in Europe (CERN, INFN, STFC, HZB, CNRS) and worldwide (FNAL, JLab). Through these links, the MACHAFILM platform supported two remote-access TA projects in the previous reporting periods.

In P3, MACHAFILM hosted four TA projects, delivering 467 AUs and thus completing the 755 AUs planned in the GA. Two projects were carried out with DESY: a cavity test (Marc Wenskat, DESY; 60 AUs), scheduled for 19/03--01/04/2026 and completed in August 2026 owing to a delay in the delivery of the cavity, and a quadrupole resonator (QPR) campaign in collaboration with HZB (Marc Wenskat, DESY, and Oliver Kugeler, HZB; 104 AUs), which started in June 2025 and was completed in July 2026, as the QPR was received from HZB only in May 2026. Two further projects, both starting on 01/10/2025, were carried out for STFC: cavity tests (143 AUs), completed in July 2026 when the cavities were shipped back after delays in the delivery of hardware components from STFC, and tunnelling spectroscopy and surface characterisation of STFC samples (160 AUs), completed in March 2026.

As part of the service improvements, new instrumentation for magnetometry and tunnelling spectroscopy (including Cernox temperature sensors) was installed at MACHAFILM, and X-ray diffraction fitting software was developed to extract the grain size and strain parameters of user samples. In addition, dedicated software to model the behaviour of liquid helium and the mechanical properties of materials at cryogenic temperatures was purchased for the CRYOMECH platform. These additions were used in the TA tests and extended the facility's capabilities.

\subparagraph{XBOX} \mbox{}

The XBOX facility hosts klystron-based X-band test stands located at CERN. These test stands are dedicated to the testing and development of high-gradient accelerating structures and high-power RF components, initially developed for the CLIC project, but also applicable to R\&D for X-band FELs, Compton/Thomson sources, and potential RF units for linear accelerators. As a result, XBOX offers unique, state-of-the-art yet highly specialized capabilities, which makes it challenging to meet a broader and more diverse needs of a wider external user community.
Despite sustained publicity efforts during the reference period, the XBOX facility did not attract any new transnational access (TA) projects. 

\subparagraph{Task 3.3 : Electron and Plasma Beams} \mbox{}

% \todo{Briefly explain the progress of the task in context to the DoA.}

This task includes six facilities located across four laboratories, offering access to electron or plasma beams.

During P3, the four operational facilities hosted 15 TA projects and delivered a total of 2,100 AUs. KIT-KARA was the main contributor: thanks to the reallocation of resources, it delivered more AUs than initially planned in the GA. The second KIT facility, FLUTE, hosted one project before being dismantled for an upgrade. INFN-BTF was also active, hosting four TA projects, while the UHI100 facility at CEA-LIDYL overcame the administrative issues related to its operating licence and was able to host a project just before the end of EURO-LABS. By contrast, unexpected incidents prevented any access to the CLARA facility in the UK, despite the preparations made and the expectations for its use. 

Beyond the TA projects, all Service Improvements, mainly at KIT-KARA, were successfully completed and are already being used.

Details for each facility are provided in the following paragraphs.

\subsubsection*{Main Results and Achievements}

%\todo{Briefly summarise the main results and achievements of the WP in context of the DoA.}

\subparagraph{INFN-LNF} \mbox{}

The Frascati National Laboratories (LNF) of the Italian Institute for Nuclear Physics (INFN) offer TA through two facilities: BTF (Beam Test Facility) and SPARC\_LAB (Sources for Plasma Accelerators and Radiation Compton with Laser And Beam).

\textbf{BTF} is mainly dedicated to the development and testing of particle detectors. It provides a primary beam of fixed energy and secondary electron or positron beams with continuously tunable energy from \SI{30}{\MeV} to \SI{780}{\MeV} and multiplicity from $10^{10}$ particles/pulse down to a single particle/pulse in a Poisson stochastic regime.

\textbf{SPARC\_LAB} combines the high-brightness SPARC photo-injector, providing electron beams with energies up to \SI{180}{\MeV}, with the high-intensity FLAME laser, which generates infrared laser pulses with an intensity of $10^{19}$~W/cm$^2$. The facility is mainly devoted to R\&D on novel acceleration techniques, advanced diagnostics and the generation of radiation ranging from THz to extreme UV light.

During P3, BTF operated smoothly and regularly and hosted four TA projects, described below. SPARC\_LAB, by contrast, did not provide any TA during the project. Its operation was limited by a total of six months of shutdown, needed to carry out a consolidation and upgrade programme and to install SABINA, a new facility for FEL radiation production in the THz range. This programme was partly funded by the regional government and followed a fixed timeline that could not be postponed. In addition, SPARC\_LAB is the test stand for the R\&D on plasma acceleration that underpins EuPRAXIA, the laboratory's flagship project, and this work had the highest priority during operation. Together, the installation of the new facility and the priority given to the internal scientific programme left no room for TA slots. The resources planned for SPARC\_LAB were therefore reallocated to BTF.

Serving a broad user community, both in terms of research fields and of national and international origin, BTF delivered 1,176 AUs, a figure equal to the total planned in the GA for the whole project. Thanks to the resources reallocated from SPARC\_LAB, INFN-BTF closed the project having delivered 157\% of its planned AUs. The four experiments were: \textit{Pcube\_DIAG\_TEST}, the characterisation of a PSI-Pcube diagnostics chamber prototype (view screen and scintillating fibre) as a function of the charge and energy of the BTF e$^-$/e$^+$ beam; \textit{CoMOMDeMPE}, a detector control system for managing physics experiments; \textit{MULTICHECK}, a study of the multiplicity induced in charged-particle detectors by passive and active construction and environmental elements; and \textit{PIXSTREAM}, the testing, calibration and synchronisation of silicon pixel detectors as a function of beam multiplicity, geometry and energy, as part of R\&D on novel detectors for future colliders. 


% \separatorline{0.3em}{0.5\textwidth}{0.4pt}

\subparagraph{KIT-ALFA} \mbox{}

KIT-ALFA (Accelerator Facilities) offers TA through two facilities: the Karlsruhe Research Accelerator (KARA), a 110-m storage ring and 2.5-GeV synchrotron radiation source, and FLUTE, a compact far-infrared linear accelerator and test experiment and an ultra-short-pulse terahertz (THz) source.

\textbf{KARA} During P3, KARA operated regularly and delivered 788 AUs, bringing its total since the start of the project to 1,606 AUs, i.e. 182\% of the AUs planned in the GA, thanks to the resources reallocated from other facilities. It hosted nine TA projects, some of which returned to the facility for a second beam time, covering beam-based alignment for FCC-ee, beam stability experiments with beam-based alignment, RDP studies for FCC-ee, measurements of the photoelectron yield of accelerator vacuum material surfaces, and the analysis of photon-stimulated electron emission from Cu beam screens and functional a-C coatings for electron-cloud mitigation.

\textbf{FLUTE}: FLUTE was planned to join the EURO-LABS TA offer later in the project because of scheduled upgrades. Further delays in these activities reduced the time available before the facility was dismantled in spring 2026, to be reinstalled beyond the duration of EURO-LABS as the injector for the cSTART ring. FLUTE was nevertheless able to host a single project, on a novel compact transverse deflecting system based on split-ring resonators (Compact-TDS: ISSR and TSR) for longitudinal electron bunch diagnostics, delivering 120 AUs, i.e. 36.5\% of its planned AUs. The resources freed up were reallocated to the service improvements at the facility.

\textbf{Service Improvements:} all the service improvements planned at KIT-ALFA were completed, and the new software tools were tested during some of the TA experiments carried out at KARA.

\underline{\em{Simulation and Measurement Framework}}: the online optics simulation based on Ocelot was extended with scripts for the automated measurement of accelerator parameters such as chromaticity, dispersion, beta function and response matrix, and tools for beam-based alignment were developed together with a TA project. For camera image processing, several open-source tools were evaluated and areaDetector\footnote{\url{https://areadetector.github.io/areaDetector/index.html}} was implemented at KARA and FLUTE. KIT also joined the Python Accelerator Middle Layer collaboration\footnote{\url{https://github.com/python-accelerator-middle-layer}} to benefit from the shared development effort. In addition, a Python measurement framework was developed to turn measurement scripts, often written ad hoc by scientists, students or operators, into reusable tools. Users only implement the preparation, the measurement loop and the post-measurement analysis, while the framework automatically generates a command-line interface, a GUI and an EPICS IOC with operator panels (CSS/Phoebus), saves the data and analysis results in HDF5 files, handles exceptions, allows measurements to be aborted and restores the machine state afterwards, without requiring any knowledge of the control system.

\underline{\em{Data Management Framework}}: following an in-depth comparison of three research data management platforms, B2Share\footnote{\url{https://b2share.eudat.eu/}}, RADAR\footnote{\url{https://radar.products.fiz-karlsruhe.de/en}} and Kadi4Mat\footnote{\url{https://kadi.iam.kit.edu/}}, Kadi4Mat was selected. Developed at KIT, it is an open-source platform that combines an electronic lab notebook with a data repository and supports FAIR data practices. Records describing data, samples or devices can be grouped into collections, linked to each other and shared with users and groups through role-based permissions. Two instances were deployed: an internal one (\url{kadi.ibpt.kit.edu}), accessed with KIT accounts and connected to the KIT Large Scale Data Facility (LSDF) for large datasets, and an external read-only copy (\url{kadi-www.ibpt.kit.edu}), where external users log in with their ORCID account to access public data. The configuration was optimised for data transfer and security, and dedicated instances can be replicated for partners and collaborators.

% \separatorline{0.3em}{0.5\textwidth}{0.4pt}

\subparagraph{STFC-CLARA} \mbox{}

CLARA (Compact Linear Accelerator for Research and Applications), located at the STFC Daresbury Laboratory in the UK, is a high-performance, modular injector facility delivering a highly stable and customisable short-pulse electron beam to a series of test enclosures. Its principal aim is to test advanced free-electron laser (FEL) schemes that can later be implemented on existing and future short-wavelength FELs, and it is designed to support research on beam dynamics and on accelerator technology challenges in photoinjectors, RF acceleration, timing and synchronisation, beam diagnostics, controls and feedback. Electrons are accelerated to \SI{250}{\MeV} by three 4-m-long, \SI{3}{\GHz} RF accelerating structures. In addition, the Full Energy Beam Exploitation (FEBE) beamline features a 120-TW ultra-short-pulse laser, enabling unique experiments that combine high-power lasers with bright electron beams to probe novel accelerator concepts.

CLARA did not provide any TA during the project. It was understood from the start that the facility would not be immediately available to TA users, but it was expected to open approximately one year into EURO-LABS. However, significant technical delays and reconfigurations of the facility, together with changing priorities in the UK science sector, substantially lengthened the commissioning period. Finally, serious safety incidents during commissioning prevented any access until the end of EURO-LABS, even though several international users had been preparing applications to use the facility.

% \separatorline{0.3em}{0.5\textwidth}{0.4pt}

\subparagraph{LPA UHI100} \mbox{}

One beamtime experiment (CEA-LIDYL-2026-LPA-UHI100-1) was scheduled at LPA-UHI100 for May–June 2026, following the granting of the facility's operating licence by CEA in January 2026, based on authorisation from the Nuclear Safety Authority (ASNR) in December 2025. The licence permits the production of electron bunches with energies up to \SI{200}{MeV}, charges up to \SI{300}{pC}, and a repetition rate of \SI{0.3}{Hz}.

The experiment was scheduled as late as possible within the EURO-LABS project timeline due to persistent cooling-water circuit issues affecting the laser and experimental areas. Although these issues were resolved in May 2026, a major air-conditioning failure interrupted the experiment during its first week, shortly after completion of most of the experimental setup. Consequently, only 16 of the 192 planned Access Units (AU) could be delivered. The system was repaired in early July, but the external users were no longer available to resume the experiment.

Nevertheless, EURO-LABS facilitated valuable new collaborations that are expected to continue through future projects.

\textbf{Service Improvements:} As part of the service improvements, a second turbomolecular pump was installed at the LPA-UHI100 facility to increase the repetition rate and the number of shots available to users per day, while supporting machine-learning-based electron beam optimisation (WP5, Task 5.3). The upgraded system was initially tested at a maximum repetition rate of \SI{0.12}{Hz}.

Budget reallocation towards the end of the project enabled further improvements to the gas delivery system, including modifications to the valves and piping and the introduction of a purge phase. These upgrades improved gas delivery to the target and residual gas evacuation from the interaction chamber through the two pumping systems and additional purge lines. As a result, the facility achieved a repetition rate of 0.3 Hz, the maximum authorised by ASNR, significantly increasing its operational capacity and the number of shots available to users.

\subparagraph*{INCT/RAPID} 
During P3, INCT/RAPID hosted ten TA projects supported by EURO-LABS, including the repetition of an experiment first carried out in P1, and delivered 405 AUs. The experiments covered a wide range of R\&D areas, from fundamental studies to applications, some of them exploring new capabilities of the facility.

Several projects addressed food and agriculture: the effect of low-energy electron beam (LEEB) irradiation on micro-organisms in livestock feed, the optimisation of LEEB food-irradiation processes supported by Monte Carlo dosimetry, and electron-beam mutagenesis of barley seeds to improve their resilience to abiotic stress, together with the conservation of barley and wheat grains and flours. Polymer and materials research included the radiation synthesis of PVP-based nanogels and polymer nanohydrogels for drug delivery and theranostics, the crosslinking of self-assembled fatty acids on copper, the effect of irradiation on sheep-wool biopolymer composites for insulation and packaging, and a systematic comparison of gamma and electron-beam irradiation of polyethylene and polypropylene, producing a dataset for machine-learning applications. Other projects studied the conversion of CO$_2$ and CH$_4$ into syngas through electron-beam-driven methane dry reforming, and used electron nuclear double resonance spectroscopy to study Li$_4$SiO$_4$--Li$_2$TiO$_3$ ceramic breeder pebbles for fusion reactors after irradiation with \SI{10}{\MeV} electrons.

\subparagraph*{CERN-CLEAR} \mbox{}

CLEAR is used extensively for research on future electron accelerators, alongside a rigorous research programme on FLASH radiotherapy, and has contributed to all three hands-on EURO-LABS Training Schools. During P3, the facility remained operational and hosted one additional TA project, delivering 40 AUs and bringing its cumulative total to 476 AUs, i.e.\ 40\% of the AUs planned in the GA.

\textbf{Service Improvements:} the service improvements planned at CLEAR aim to improve the quality of measurements and data acquisition in areas critical for a large part of the users, and to increase the number of users that can access the facility per unit time. The main improvement is a new beam line, to which EURO-LABS contributed, in particular for the associated instrumentation. The new line allows a faster turn-around of experiments by minimising installation time, and provides an end-of-line experimental station with greater beam flexibility and improved handling and measurement hardware. The beam line was installed at the end of 2025, commissioned in 2026 and has since been used for experiments (see Figure~\ref{fig:wp3-clear-2ndbl-inst}).

The items purchased with EURO-LABS funds, mainly for the end-of-line experimental area, include a beam current measurement device (ICT), an optical table, vacuum and optical components, beam screens and associated cameras, as well as drawing-office work; a new version of the C-robot sample handler was also designed, built and tested. EURO-LABS funds were further allocated to an ionisation chamber for real-time dosimetry, two vacuum chambers for the dipoles of the new beam line, and ADC, timing and other data acquisition hardware.
