\subparagraph{Task 5.2: Open Science } \mbox{}

Within WP5, the goal of the Task 5.2 is to provide new services to the users in order to achieve an improved and facilitated access to experimental datasets produced in Research Infrastructures. 
Actions have been put in place to ensure that data generated at the various RIs are treated in line with the FAIR principles, following a proper Data Management Plan aligned with international best practices, including those recommended by the \textbf{European Open Science Cloud (EOSC)} and the \textbf{FAIRsFAIR} project. This aim to ensure that the data not only meet technical standards but also support the broader goals of open science. The new services aim at improved FAIR data practices. This includes (1) a new data catalog (openNP), (2) an authentication and authorization (AAI) service and (3) a prototype of platform for data access.

During the reporting period, for the sub-tasks, the main results are :
\begin{itemize}
  
  \item The successful launch of the \textbf{openNP catalogue} (\url{https://opennp.in2p3.fr}), which provided researchers with a user-friendly, metadata-rich repository for experimental datasets in nuclear physics. The catalogue was not only a discovery tool but also a gateway to high-quality, FAIR-compliant data, enabling researchers to identify, access, and reuse datasets with ease. The design, architecture, and operational framework of openNP were thoroughly documented in \textbf{Deliverable D5.3} (\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17020290}) and \textbf{Deliverable D5.6} (\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22687267}). These deliverables outlined the interoperability standards, metadata schemas, and FAIR assessment metrics adopted by the catalogue, ensuring its compatibility with existing and future RIs. To guarantee the long-term sustainability and relevance of openNP, a governing board was established, comprising five representatives from leading European institutions: GANIL (France), GSI (Germany), CNA (Spain), INFN (Italy), and LPCCaen (France). This board oversaw strategic decision-making, including policy development, community engagement, and the prioritization of future enhancements, ensuring that openNP continued to evolve in response to user needs and emerging standards in open science and data management.
  \item The deployment of the \textbf{AAI service} (\url{https://iam-eurolabs.ijclab.in2p3.fr/}), which simplified and standardized user authentication across multiple platforms. This service significantly reduced the administrative overhead for both users and service providers by implementing a single sign-on (SSO) solution that eliminated the need for multiple credentials. By the end of the project, the AAI service had been integrated into several key platforms, including Theo4Exp (Task 2.4 of EURO-LABS), a Virtual Access platform for theoretical nuclear physics that began using the service to authenticate its users. The AAI was also adopted by openNP to enable secure and role-based access to its datasets, as well as by Grafana monitoring services at GSI and GANIL, where operational teams gained access to dashboards and tools with granular permissions. The service amassed over 250 registered users by the project's conclusion, and promotional activities, such as workshops and direct engagement with RI communities, were undertaken to raise awareness and encourage broader adoption. The AAI service not only improved the user experience by reducing friction for researchers but also enhanced security by centralizing authentication and authorization processes.
  \item Different data lake prototypes are being developed/tested, also in coordination with the EU ESCAPE Project and the PUNCH4NFDI (consortium of particle, astro-, astroparticle, hadron and nuclear physics, representing about 9.000 scientists with a Ph.D. in Germany) project. The results from these activities, combined with the other related efforts like AAI, will lead to a federated infrastructure concept that will be implemented in GSI/FAIR and other facilities. 
  %infrastructures.
  \item Two advanced training schools were organized, focusing on open science principles, collaborative software development, and data management best practices. These schools targeted early-career researchers, data stewards, and IT professionals, providing hands-on training in FAIR data curation, metadata standards, and tooling for reproducible research, including version control, containerization, and workflow management. The training also covered the practical use of openNP and AAI, ensuring that participants could apply these services in their own work. The schools reached a wide audience and fostered a culture of open science and collaborative data management within the community. Feedback from participants indicated a high level of satisfaction, with many reporting that they had gained practical skills
  of immediate application in their research.
%  that they could immediately apply in their research.
\end{itemize}
     


Finally, all digital objects produced by the project, including reports, deliverables, training materials, and communications, were archived in the \textbf{EURO-LABS Zenodo community} (\url{https://zenodo.org/communities/euro-labs}). This ensured long-term accessibility, citability, and reuse in accordance with open science principles. The sustainability of the services developed under Task 5.2 remained a major priority throughout the project. The governing board for openNP, the growing user base of the AAI service, and the ongoing collaboration with EU ESCAPE and PUNCH4NFDI all contributed to the long-term viability of these initiatives. The federated infrastructure concept, in particular, gained traction as it was piloted in GSI/FAIR and other RIs, paving the way for a more connected and interoperable data landscape in European physics research. The successes of Task 5.2 demonstrated the value of a coordinated, user-centric approach to data management and access, and the lessons learned will continue to inform future efforts in this area.


\subparagraph{Task 5.3: Machine Learning} \mbox{} 
%(Month xx-yy)} 

%\todo{Work in Progress}

Within WP5, the Task 5.3 aims to develop Machine Learning (ML) methods to improve beam characteristics, transport efficiency, and reproducibility of accelerator tuning, which will reduce the beam preparation time and thus increase the available time for the experiments. Accelerator laboratories worldwide are exploring a large variety of techniques to achieve this aim, from classical optimization and Bayesian optimization (BO) to reinforcement learning. One part of this automation effort is to provide a framework that allows machine experts, operators, and users to solve certain, focused optimization problems and to make these solutions reusable in an operational context. We call this project the “Generic Optimization Framework and Frontend”, or GeOFF for short.  EURO-LABS financed one scientific staff member at GSI for three years for deployment and implication of GeOFF for improved beam delivery at EURO-LABS facilities.

The main result of Task 5.3 in RP3 is the continued rollout of new optimization approaches. The successful application of Geoff, incorporating these new methods to improve accelerator tuning at GSI, has been confirmed in several dedicated beam experiments and published in Appel et al., EPJ Research Infrastructures (2026) https://doi.org/10.1007/s41781-026-00175-6. This work includes a newly developed, much faster optimization approach (under 5 minutes) for the target area, where only limited phase-space information is available (see Figure~\ref{fig:wp5_frs}). GeOFF has also been successfully applied in dedicated ion-source setups for the PUMA experiment at TU Darmstadt and for PUMA at CERN in end of 2025 and May 2026, showing that real-time tuning of ion sources and particle traps is achievable. In particular, GeOFF’s high flexibility—enabling integration of both new and legacy control systems—has markedly eased and sped up the adoption of ML methods at GSI and previously at CERN.

The BTS and ATS schools were fully organized and financed from this project, while those co-sponsored were only financed in part (usually “seed money”), while also logistically supported by our community. Each event had its own website, and all submitted reports were fully or partially included in the EURO-LABS Newsletters. 

The BTS and ATS schools were all hands-on events and were very much appreciated by the students, at both levels. The students originated from each of the 3 initial communities that form EURO-LABS, were very diverse and with good gender equilibrium. Some of the organizers underline that this equilibrium has happened naturally, was not biased by the selection process; we take this as a proof of the present situation of the community. In both cases the selection was based on the profile of the applicants, their motivation and the recommendations of their scientific advisors. This was not an easy task as the rate of applications was more than 2 to 1.  Participants’ appreciations were clearly expressed as “excellent” in their anonymous responses to organizers’ questionnaires.

 The organizing institutions of the BTS 2025 (CNA Seville) and 2026 (IFIN-HH Bucharest) had the common and important feature that they host several (similar) diverse installations, based on high technologies, but appropriate to easy hands-on approaches by students (under supervision) and for applications. Hands-on was the basic principle: the students visited and worked at target-making labs, attended radiocarbon dating at AMS tandetrons, Ion Beam Analysis and nuclear astrophysics (NA) experiments at the 3 MV tandetrons, and nuclear spectroscopy at larger accelerators. Extended visits to other interesting installations in the same institutions were also appreciated due to the possibly of extending students’ direct contact with the scientific world of our community %works on. 
 Organizers invited only a few (2-3) young, but accomplished scientists for lectures, and at BTS26 a few sponsors were invited to present topics like vacuum and detectors.

 The advanced ATS schools addressed two different domains: on one hand the steps to steer beam in different facilities, such as electrons in CLEAR, protons in the ring of the PSBooster , heavy ions at ISOLDE and proton deceleration at the Antiproton Decelerator (AD) at CERN. A summary of the success of the ATSOA school was presented in the international Particle Accelerator Conference (IPAC 2026).  On the other hand, as in the previous reporting period, a school was dedicated to topics newly important for our community, like data management plans. In each event the organizers added social events that allowed the time and conditions for the participants to know each-other better, to know the culture and landscapes of the hosting country or region.  Fig.~\ref{fig:ATSOA-summary} gives a summary, on the left plot, of the level of the participants in the four ATSOA schools and shows, on the right, the distribution by nationalities of the ATSOA participants.

All information about the  schools can be found on the EURO-LABS web site. 

In addition, TSB and the leadership of our work group came to the conclusion that it was possible and worthy of our attention to support training opportunities that originated from various other groups in the wider European community of science. We could, therefore, co-sponsor events organized across Europe, namely events that are traditional and with well-established prestige, like the NIC school, La Rabida or the Carpathian schools, but also ad-hoc events that were organized due to the perceived need of specific topics, like the R-matrix, FAST26, Cluster 2026 and the CERN instrumentation school. Funds provided to those events were used to expand the attendance of students and young scientists to events otherwise not accessible to them. 


In conclusion, considering the experience acquired by setting task 5.4 and from the discussions of the community at the General Assemblies where we reported, we dare to say that {\bf we highly recommend, for future European projects, to include explicitly and allocate appropriate funds for the training} of current members or future members of our community. In addition to funds to be used

The experience of the four years of Task 5.4 of EURO-LABS shows that the cost for this is small in comparison to the results.
