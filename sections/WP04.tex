\subsubsection*{Status}
WP4 of EURO-LABS is devoted to support detector R\&D via Transnational Access (TA) carried out at 11 RIs in Europe. The typical detector R\&D cycle consists of constructing a series of prototypes, testing them in high-energy particle beams, irradiating them and possibly evaluating their performance in special characterization facilities. This is followed by the division of WP4 into tasks: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item WP4.1 supplies the test beams in 3 RIs, 
    \item WP4.2 offers detector characterization in 2 RIs and
    \item WP4.3 irradiation opportunities in 6 RIs.
\end{itemize}

The \textbf{CERN} PS (Proton Synchrotron) and SPS (Super Proton Synchrotron) test beams provide particle beams in the energy range from ~1 GeV to ~350 GeV. Upstream of the physicist’s test setup sophisticated beam line equipment allows selecting the type, polarity and energy of particles as well as the beam intensity. A total of seven general purpose test beam lines and their large, well-equipped experimental areas are available for detector R\&D.
%\end{prtwo}

%\begin{prtwo}
\textbf{DESY} (Deutsches Elektronen-Synchrotron) at Hamburg presently operates several particle accelerators of worldwide relevance including PETRA III, FLASH and the European XFEL. The DESY II synchrotron is used as an injector for PETRA III and delivers in parallel electron or positron beams for up to three test beam areas using a fixed secondary target. As the demand on the PETRA III light source is high, DESY II operates with a very high availability of >99\%. DESY II can provide electron beams with an energy between 1 GeV and 6 GeV, a small energy spread of about 5\%, and rates up to several 10 kHz, depending on beam line and the choice of the secondary target. 
%\end{prtwo}

%\begin{prtwo}
The Paul Scherrer Institut (\textbf{PSI}) is a multidisciplinary Swiss national research laboratory. Among other accelerators it runs the high intensity proton accelerator (HIPA) complex which delivers protons to the Swiss research infrastructure for particle physics (CHRISP). Both installations of WP4.1.3, PiM1 and UCN, belong to CHRISP and serve both, physics experiments and test beam areas for detector qualification. The secondary beamline PiM1 is designed to provide pions with a very well-defined momentum between 100 and 500 MeV/c. However, also a beam operation mode for electrons and muons exists. The beam profile and particle flux are adjustable by the users. The beam is continuous with a bunch frequency of 50MHz. The ultracold neutron facility (UCN) has 2 beamlines (West-1, West-2). West-1 provides a world-leading UCN intensity and is operated in a pulsed mode with UCN pulses every 300s, or at adjustable longer intervals or lower intensity on user request. West-2 provides about a factor 10 lower intensity.

\subsubsection*{Reports from WP4 RIs}

The following subsections summarize activities at the research infrastructures (RIs) providing Transnational Access within Tasks 4.1--4.3 during Reporting Period 3 (RP3). The emphasis is on access delivered during RP3 and on representative scientific highlights. A detailed report on the AU delivery during the complete EURO-LABS project is provided in deliverable D4.1 (https://web.infn.it/EURO-LABS/results-deliverables/). 

\subparagraph{Sub-task 4.1.1 CERN PS\&SPS} \mbox{}
Two campaigns were selected among the 18 projects executed at the CERN PS\&SPS test beams in RP3: the CERN DRD3 Collaboration programme on solid-state detectors and the ALICE ITS3 tracker upgrade.

During the 2025 and 2026 run periods at the CERN SPS H6B beamline, DRD3 coordinated multi-user test-beam campaigns focused on next-generation detector concepts, in particular 4D tracking combining precision position measurement with ultra-fast timing. Shared use of common infrastructure allowed main and parasitic users to operate in parallel and substantially increased the scientific output per allocated beam period. A standardized AIDAinnova telescope, cold-box and chiller infrastructure was deployed and optimized, and after the complete dismantling of the AIDA telescope at the end of 2025, a functional five-plane telescope was rebuilt in March 2026. The common DUT data-acquisition framework was upgraded in mid-2026, increasing sustained data-taking rates.

More than 15 institutes from nine countries participated in the DRD3 campaigns. Scientific highlights included the validation of monolithic CMOS timing sensors, measurements of trench-isolated LGADs, and comparative beam tests of DC- and AC-coupled resistive silicon detectors. The Mini-Cactus devices demonstrated timing performance at the level of a few tens of picoseconds (Figure~\ref{fig:cactus}), while trench-isolated LGADs showed good spatial homogeneity even after irradiation (Figure~\ref{fig:tilgad}). The resistive detector programme achieved spatial resolutions of only a few percent of the pitch while retaining LGAD-like timing performance (Figure~\ref{fig:rsd}) %\cite{aidainnova,terzo2026,gonzalez2026,arcidiacono2026,menzio2026}
.

\subparagraph{Sub-task 4.1.2 DESY} \mbox{}
During RP3, the DESY II Test Beam facility provided transnational access to 21 TA project groups, involving 102 users and delivering 4776 AUs. Two representative projects illustrate the breadth of the programme.

The project \emph{X-ray transition radiation by high-energy electrons in external magnetic field} investigated the effect of a magnetic field on the spectral properties of X-ray transition radiation. Measurements at the TB21 beamline with 4 and 5~GeV electrons provided absolute X-ray synchrotron-radiation spectra and enabled tests of the interplay between transition and synchrotron radiation. A multi-foil target was constructed and preliminary spectra were collected in the optimized detector geometry. Results were presented at international conferences and stimulated further theoretical work.

A second project, \emph{Investigation towards compensated LGAD sensors for extreme fluences}, characterized n-type LGAD sensors with emphasis on charge multiplication, spatial uniformity, noise and timing. The campaign compared nLGADs with conventional p-type LGADs and included samples irradiated up to $5\times10^{14}$~n$_{\mathrm{eq}}$/cm$^2$. The measurements showed radiation-induced donor deactivation correlated with a reduction of charge multiplication and provided new data relevant to the development of radiation-hard silicon timing detectors. The test-beam setup is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:desysetup}.

\subparagraph{Sub-task 4.1.3 PSI} \mbox{}
At PSI, the availability of the PiM1 beamline for detector tests remained constrained by the MUSE physics programme. In 2025, two supported test-beam campaigns were carried out. A notable highlight was the PIONEER calorimeter study using LYSO crystals. The campaign demonstrated that the proposed crystal technology satisfies the experiment's requirements and supported the decision to adopt LYSO as the baseline calorimeter technology. The 2026 PiM1 detector-test period started only in September, after the end of EURO-LABS, so no further RP3 TA could be supported there. The UCN facility continued to support detector tests relevant to fundamental neutron physics, including counters and scintillation detectors used in precision experiments.

\subparagraph{Sub-task 4.2.1 RBI} \mbox{}
The RBI Tandem Accelerator Facility supported five RP3 user projects covering detector materials, diamond devices, thin foils and silicon instrumentation for space applications. Most projects used in-vacuum or in-air Ion Beam Induced Charge (IBIC) imaging, with focused ion microbeams providing micrometre or sub-micrometre spatial resolution.

One highlight investigated avalanche mechanisms in single-crystal CVD diamond structures fabricated at CEA-LIST. Planar and vertical-field-plate Schottky diode geometries were irradiated with 8~MeV carbon ions and 7.5--10~MeV oxygen ions. Full-area IBIC scans showed localized uncontrolled charge amplification at the edge of the contact metallization, consistent with electric-field crowding and onset of avalanche multiplication before the nominal bulk field reached the corresponding value. The device structures and an example IBIC map are shown in Figures~\ref{fig:rbidiodes} and \ref{fig:rbiibic}.

Two further campaigns supported development of the SP@M energetic-particle spectrometer for the proposed ESA M-MATISSE mission. Controlled proton and alpha irradiations of thick silicon detectors established the first accelerator reference data for charge-collection and pulse-shape studies. Comparing junction-side and ohmic-side irradiation demonstrated that the latter configuration provides clear electron/ion separation from approximately 400~keV, supporting compact particle identification with a single silicon detector. These results were presented at PIXEL2026.

\subparagraph{Sub-task 4.2.2 ITAINNOVA} \mbox{}
Within EURO-LABS, the EMC laboratory at ITAINNOVA performs standard and advanced electromagnetic-compatibility testing of detector electronics, addressing electromagnetic emissions and susceptibility under realistic operating conditions. The measurements provide information on noise-coupling mechanisms, grounding strategies, sensitive frequency regions, filter design and power-supply emission requirements. During the project, systems characterized at the laboratory included electronics for pixel detectors, Micromegas-based detectors and timing detectors.

A major RP3 activity was the EMC characterization of the ETROC2 timing ASIC developed for the CMS Endcap Timing Layer. Given the stringent timing requirements, the campaign investigated the effect of conducted electromagnetic disturbances on noise, timing stability and data integrity. Noise was injected through different coupling ports of the ETROC2 test board over the range from 100~kHz to 100~MHz. The response depended strongly on the injection point, with the analogue and digital power lines (VDDA and VDDD) and the clock inputs identified as the most critical coupling paths. Increasing disturbance amplitude and frequency produced increased noise width and baseline fluctuations, while propagation through the power-distribution network could affect supply stability and PLL locking. The setup and the measured channel-to-channel noise response are shown in Figures~\ref{fig:itaetrocsetup} and \ref{fig:itaetrocnoise}.

A second RP3 activity applied the detector-characterization methodology to LESA, an autonomous solar-powered IoT sensing system for high-resolution environmental and agricultural monitoring. Because several LESA measurements rely on small changes in charge and capacitance, the campaign assessed the robustness of individual measurement channels against electromagnetic disturbances rather than performing only conventional pass/fail EMC tests. Radiated emissions, radiated immunity, conducted immunity and electrostatic-discharge tests were carried out while continuously monitoring the system response (Figure~\ref{fig:italesa}).

Although radiated-emission results were satisfactory for the tested configuration, functional disturbances were observed during radiated-immunity, conducted-immunity and ESD testing. A particularly sensitive frequency range was identified between 280 and 320~MHz at 10~V/m. Correlating the applied disturbance with the sensor response produced actionable information for improving filtering, shielding, interconnections and electronic design. The campaign also demonstrates the transfer of HEP detector-characterization methods to other technological domains.

\subparagraph{Sub-task 4.3.1 CERN IRRAD} \mbox{}

The samples included thin sensors and compensated LGADs, in which boron and phosphorus implants are combined to improve tolerance against acceptor removal. \subparagraph{Sub-task 4.3.2 CERN GIF++} \mbox{}
GIF++ combines a high-activity $^{137}$Cs gamma source with muon or pion test beams on the CERN SPS H4 beamline. During RP3, the facility underwent a major bunker extension in 2025. The new layout improves beam exploitation, in particular for complete scans of detector systems with areas above 1~m$^2$, and the gas area was enlarged to support the growing number of users. The facility routinely hosts more than seven setups in parallel, reaching a peak of 15 in July 2026 during the last test-beam period before LS3.

The principal R\&D programme remains detector qualification for the HL-LHC, including long-term ageing studies. The search for environmentally friendly gas mixtures has become an important component of the scientific programme through the ECOGAS collaboration. During LS3, when SPS beam is unavailable, GIF++ will continue operating in irradiation-only mode.

\subparagraph{Sub-task 4.3.3 JSI TRIGA} \mbox{}
The Ljubljana TRIGA reactor continued to provide neutron irradiations over an exceptionally broad fluence range. In RP3, 34 irradiation projects were executed, corresponding to 367 AUs. The programme supported final-stage HL-LHC detector development as well as R\&D for future experiments, with strong activity on LGAD variants, monolithic CMOS detectors, SiC and other wide-bandgap materials.

In collaboration with DRD3, EURO-LABS organized a second extreme-fluence campaign in 2025. Samples from ten groups in Europe, the USA and China were irradiated, including Si, SiC, GaN and diamond devices. Fluences up to $10^{18}$~n$_{\mathrm{eq}}$/cm$^2$ were reached, requiring long reactor cycles, special post-irradiation handling and extended cooling periods. Results already include measurements of carrier-recombination and diffusion properties in GaN and low-field mobility in silicon at extreme fluence.

Particularly promising results were obtained with trench 3D silicon detectors. Devices irradiated to $10^{17}$~n$_{\mathrm{eq}}$/cm$^2$ retained charge-collection distributions close to those measured before irradiation, while test-beam measurements showed high minimum-ionizing-particle efficiency. TCT measurements remained possible even after irradiations to $5\times10^{17}$ and $10^{18}$~n$_{\mathrm{eq}}$/cm$^2$. Figure~\ref{fig:jsi3d} shows representative charge and efficiency measurements

\subparagraph{Sub-task 4.3.4 IFJ-PAN} \mbox{}
The AIC-144 cyclotron facility provides proton beams with energies up to 60~MeV and beam currents up to 100~nA in two experimental areas. One is the former proton-therapy room, equipped with precise sample positioning and irradiation capabilities developed for clinical applications, with beam diameters up to 40~mm. The second is an experimental hall with a beamline equipped with a two-dimensional scanning system developed as part of the EURO-LABS service improvements, allowing irradiation of larger samples over an area up to $400\times400$~mm$^2$. Both irradiation stations have comprehensive dosimetry instrumentation with metrological traceability through the Secondary Standard Dosimetry Laboratory in Warsaw.

During RP3 (M31--M48), ten experiments were conducted using proton beams from the AIC-144 cyclotron. One scientific highlight, carried out by a collaboration involving Brookhaven National Laboratory and research groups in Krak\'ow, investigated radiation-induced degradation of thin Low-Gain Avalanche Detectors (LGADs) for radiation-tolerant timing applications at the Electron-Ion Collider. Sensors with active thicknesses of 20 and 30~$\mu$m were irradiated with 30 and 60~MeV protons at fluences of approximately $10^{13}$--$10^{14}$~cm$^{-2}$. The programme quantified changes in leakage current, capacitance, gain, charge collection and timing performance as a function of sensor thickness, proton energy and accumulated fluence.

Post-irradiation measurements showed fluence-dependent changes in the I--V and C--V characteristics, gain and timing performance. The comparison of gain degradation indicates that, under the investigated conditions, 30~MeV proton irradiation produces greater bulk damage per unit proton fluence than 60~MeV irradiation (Figure~\ref{fig:ifjlgad}). The measurements provide input for the qualification of radiation-tolerant LGAD timing detectors for the EIC and, more broadly, for understanding the performance limits of thin LGADs at high radiation fluence 
%\cite{zareef2026}
.

\subparagraph{Sub-task 4.3.5 UCLouvain} \mbox{}
UCLouvain provides four complementary irradiation capabilities at the Centre de Ressources du Cyclotron: the Heavy Ion Facility (HIF), Light Ion Facility (LIF), Neutron Irradiation Facility (NIF) and a high-intensity $^{60}$Co gamma source. In EURO-LABS, most projects used HIF for Single Event Effect studies, with additional proton irradiations at LIF.

One RP3 highlight was the ion-beam radiation testing of the FastRICH ASIC, developed for the LHCb RICH upgrade. Using the broad ion cocktail available at HIF, the team mapped Single Event Upset and Single Event Latchup cross-sections as a function of Linear Energy Transfer and established the corresponding Weibull behaviour. The chip was kept active during irradiation under realistic operating conditions, allowing recovery procedures and the interaction between configuration upsets and data-stream transients to be studied in real time.

A second activity studied radiation damage in silicon detectors as part of the cross-facility hardness-factor programme. Proton irradiations at two CRC energies were combined with neutron data from other facilities, while planned post-irradiation characterization includes TCT, DLTS and TSC. The work is intended to connect macroscopic detector degradation with microscopic defect populations and improve predictive models beyond simple NIEL scaling.

\subparagraph{Sub-task 4.3.6 UoB} \mbox{}
The UoB MC40 cyclotron supported several noteworthy RP3 irradiation projects. A silicon-photonics study developed a dedicated test frame with multiple temperature sensors to quantify and control heating during high-flux proton irradiation. A second project measured proton hardness factors in silicon at 15, 20 and 27~MeV, exploiting the facility's variable beam energy and precision dosimetry. These data contribute directly to the broader HADES programme.

A further highlight was the irradiation of ATLAS High Granularity Timing Detector modules. LGAD sensors bump-bonded to ALTIROC front-end ASICs were irradiated to fluences representative of HL-LHC operation, including $2.5\times10^{15}$~n$_{\mathrm{eq}}$/cm$^2$. This was the first project to benefit from the upgraded sample-scanning system, which made it possible to irradiate large modules of roughly 2~cm by 4~cm within a single day, reducing handling and environmental-exposure risks between runs. \textbf{{Delivery of AUs}}


All the three RIs of WP4.1 exceeded their full pledge of AUs. CERN benefited from running in 2026, which was not initially foreseen. Still, their performance was exceptional, delivering 266\% of their pledge. DESY experienced a rather slow start, but their performance in P3 made up for more than teir initial deficit. PSI, despite no TA delivery in P1 and a late season in 2026 still managed to considerably exceed their pledge. One should also appreciate that the delivered AU cost in all three RIs of WP4.1 was not charged to the project.



For the two RIs of WP4.2, RBI has kept their pace throughout the project and matched their full AU pledge; based on the flow of executed projects their user pool looks relatively stable. At ITTAINNOVA, despite a large effort made to persuade the builders of detector systems to conduct the tests of their detector assemblies at this unique facility, the delivery fell short of their pledge. The corrective action taken after P2 and realized in the Grant Amendment reduced their AU allocation from 800 to 550. Still, the final score of 390 AUs delivered made them fall short of even this reduced goal.

The overall goal of AU delivery in WP4.3 was achieved with a relatively comfortable margin, the excess being clearly dominated by the two CERN-based RIs. The remaining four RIs increased their performance during P3 considerably, allowing JSI and IFJ-PAN to effectively fulfil their full pledge. Both of these irradiation facilities scheduled special, AU intense campaigns to irradiate to extreme fluences in excess of $10^{17}$ and even up to $10^{18}$~n$_{\mathrm{eq}}$/cm$^2$. UCL, surprisingly experiencing no TA in the first two periods, finally attracted several projects in P3, which almost allowed them to match their pledge. The only really meagre performance is that of UoB. Their facility experienced several failures at project start and  for the remaining up-time priority was given to their commitments to ATLAS sensor QA. So even with good performance in P3 they were not able to catch up and remained at the 50\% mark. As their financing is not through EC, no corrective actions at project level were taken.
