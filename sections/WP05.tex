\subsubsection*{Status}

These transversal activities coordinated by CSIC are progressing very well. The progress of each task is very good and the community is getting very enthusiastic about the different activities. 
The specific objectives  of WP5 include:
- foster the user diversity by engaging people of different nationalities, gender, age and level of expertise, 
- enhance communication on project opportunities and dissemination of the project results, 
- promote and accelerate the development of FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable Reusable) research data management practices within the scientific community, 
- develop tools using machine learning for accelerator control, 
- enhance the competitiveness of our research infrastructure and its technical capabilities, using machine learning techniques and organizing hands-on schools.

\subparagraph{Task 5.2: Open Science } \mbox{}

Within WP5, the goal of the Task 5.2 is to provide new services to the users in order to achieve an improved and facilitated access to experimental datasets produced in Research Infrastructures. 
Actions have been put in place to ensure that data generated at the various RIs are treated in line with the FAIR principles, following a proper Data Management Plan aligned with international best practices, including those recommended by the \textbf{European Open Science Cloud (EOSC)} and the \textbf{FAIRsFAIR} project, This aim to ensure that the data not only met technical standards but also support the broader goals of open science. The new services aim at improved FAIR data practices. This includes (1) a new data catalog (openNP), (2) an authentication and authorization (AAI) service and (3) a prototype of platform for data access.

During the reporting period, for the sub-tasks, the main results are :
\begin{itemize}
  
  \item The successful launch of the \textbf{openNP catalogue} (\url{https://opennp.in2p3.fr}), which provided researchers with a user-friendly, metadata-rich repository for experimental datasets in nuclear physics. The catalogue was not only a discovery tool but also a gateway to high-quality, FAIR-compliant data, enabling researchers to identify, access, and reuse datasets with ease. The design, architecture, and operational framework of openNP were thoroughly documented in \textbf{Deliverable D5.3} (\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17020290}) and \textbf{Deliverable D5.6} (\url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22687267}). These deliverables outlined the interoperability standards, metadata schemas, and FAIR assessment metrics adopted by the catalogue, ensuring its compatibility with existing and future RIs. To guarantee the long-term sustainability and relevance of openNP, a governing board was established, comprising five representatives from leading European institutions: GANIL (France), GSI (Germany), CNA (Spain), INFN (Italy), and LPCCaen (France). This board oversaw strategic decision-making, including policy development, community engagement, and the prioritization of future enhancements, ensuring that openNP continued to evolve in response to user needs and emerging standards in open science and data management.
  \item The deployment of the \textbf{AAI service} (\url{https://iam-eurolabs.ijclab.in2p3.fr/}), which simplified and standardized user authentication across multiple platforms. This service significantly reduced the administrative overhead for both users and service providers by implementing a single sign-on (SSO) solution that eliminated the need for multiple credentials. By the end of the project, the AAI service had been integrated into several key platforms, including Theo4Exp (Task 2.4 of EURO-LABS), a Virtual Access platform for theoretical nuclear physics that began using the service to authenticate its users. The AAI was also adopted by openNP to enable secure and role-based access to its datasets, as well as by Grafana monitoring services at GSI and GANIL, where operational teams gained access to dashboards and tools with granular permissions. The service amassed over 250 registered users by the project's conclusion, and promotional activities, such as workshops and direct engagement with RI communities, were undertaken to raise awareness and encourage broader adoption. The AAI service not only improved the user experience by reducing friction for researchers but also enhanced security by centralizing authentication and authorization processes.
  \item Different data lake prototypes are being developed/tested, also in coordination with the EU ESCAPE Project and the PUNCH4NFDI (consortium of particle, astro-, astroparticle, hadron and nuclear physics, representing about 9.000 scientists with a Ph.D. in Germany) project. The results from these activities, combined with the other related efforts like AAI, will lead to a federated infrastructure concept that will be implemented in FAIR and other infrastructures.
  \item Two advanced training schools were organized, focusing on open science principles, collaborative software development, and data management best practices. These schools targeted early-career researchers, data stewards, and IT professionals, providing hands-on training in FAIR data curation, metadata standards, and tooling for reproducible research, including version control, containerization, and workflow management. The training also covered the practical use of openNP and AAI, ensuring that participants could apply these services in their own work. The schools reached a wide audience and fostered a culture of open science and collaborative data management within the community. Feedback from participants indicated a high level of satisfaction, with many reporting that they had gained practical skills that they could immediately apply in their research.
\end{itemize}
     


Finally, all digital objects produced by the project, including reports, deliverables, training materials, and communications, were archived in the \textbf{EURO-LABS Zenodo community} (\url{https://zenodo.org/communities/euro-labs}). This ensured long-term accessibility, citability, and reuse in accordance with open science principles. The sustainability of the services developed under Task 5.2 remained a major priority throughout the project. The governing board for openNP, the growing user base of the AAI service, and the ongoing collaboration with EU ESCAPE and PUNCH4NFDI all contributed to the long-term viability of these initiatives. The federated infrastructure concept, in particular, gained traction as it was piloted in FAIR and other RIs, paving the way for a more connected and interoperable data landscape in European physics research. The successes of Task 5.2 demonstrated the value of a coordinated, user-centric approach to data management and access, and the lessons learned will continue to inform future efforts in this area.


\subparagraph{Task 5.3: Machine Learning} \mbox{} 
%(Month xx-yy)} 

%\todo{Work in Progress}

Within WP5, the Task 5.3 aims to develop Machine Learning (ML) methods to improve beam characteristics, transport efficiency, and reproducibility of accelerator tuning, which will reduce the beam preparation time and thus increase the available time for the experiments. Accelerator laboratories worldwide are exploring a large variety of techniques to achieve this aim, from classical optimization and Bayesian optimization (BO) to reinforcement learning. One part of this automation effort is to provide a framework that allows machine experts, operators, and users to solve certain, focused optimization problems and to make these solutions reusable in an operational context. We call this project the “Generic Optimization Framework and Frontend”, or GeOFF for short.  EURO-LABS finances one scientific staff member at GSI for three years for deployment and implication of GeOFF for improved beam delivery at EURO-LABS facilities.

The main result of Task 5.3 in RP3 is the continued rollout of new optimization approaches. The successful application of Geoff, incorporating these new methods to improve accelerator tuning at GSI, has been confirmed in several dedicated beam experiments and published in Appel et al., EPJ Research Infrastructures (2026) https://doi.org/10.1007/s41781-026-00175-6. This work includes a newly developed, much faster optimization approach (under 5 minutes) for the target area, where only limited phase-space information is available (see Figure~\ref{fig:wp5_frs}). GeOFF has also been successfully applied in dedicated ion-source setups for the PUMA experiment at TU Darmstadt and for PUMA at CERN in end of 2025 and May 2026, showing that real-time tuning of ion sources and particle traps is achievable. In particular, GeOFF’s high flexibility—enabling integration of both new and legacy control systems—has markedly eased and sped up the adoption of ML methods at GSI and previously at CERN.

The adaptation of GeOFF to a laser-driven particle accelerator at CEA (France) has really started in November 2024, with the arrival of Proxy KAD as a post doctoral researcher, whose contract run up to the end of September 2025. The program is ready to be used but the tests have been delayed, firstly because of the Nuclear Safety Authorization which has been delivered only early this year 2026. The test phase requires shots at full energy on target with maximum repetition rate. 
GeOFF tests were planned during a beamtime access on the source within the framework of TNA – WP3 (LPAUHI100-01-EOS), initially in may-june 2026. Unfortunately, the beamtime was significantly shortened because of a major failure of the air-conditioning system in the laser room, which remained out of service for several weeks. The first tests that have been done underline the weakness of the network architecture which was not optimized to support the whole process. The new configuration is expected to be deployed by the end of September 2026. 
The GeOFF optimization toolkit may therefore be used during autumn 2026, as the necessary infrastructure is expected to be ready by then. A source optimization campaign is planned at the LPA-UHI100 facility, providing an opportunity to conduct the first full-scale tests of the toolkit.

Organizers invited only a few (2-3) young, but accomplished scientists for lectures, and at BTS26 a few sponsors were invited to present topics like vacuum and detectors.

 The advanced ATS schools addressed two different domains on one hand the step to steer beam in different facilities, Electron in CLEAR, protons in the ring of the PSBooster , heavy ions at ISOLDE and proton deceleration at the Antiproton decelerator (AD) at CERN. A summary of the success of the ATSOA school was presented in the international Particle Accelerator Conference (IPAC 2026).  On the other hand, as in previous reporting period a school was dedicated to topics newly important for our community, like data management plans. In each event the organizers added social events that allowed the time and conditions for the participants to know each-other better, to know the culture and landscapes of the hosting country or region. The Fig.~\ref{fig:ATSOA-summary} gives a summary on the left plot of the level of the participants in the four ATSOA schools and on the right the distribution by nationalities of the ATSOA participants. 

All information about the  schools can be reached from the EURO-LABS web site. The schools organised by EURO-LABS were oversubscribed and had students from various parts of the World.  The training Scientific Board (TSB) selected in the first months of the project was involved in the selection of the candidates and in the program for theses hands-on schools. {\bf In the end, the “adult supervisors” appreciated the capabilities of the young participants very much, their curiosity, openness, their genuine interest for science in particular, and knowledge in general. We believe our community has a good future ahead of us!}

In addition, TSB and the leadership of our work group, arrived at the conclusion that it is possible and worthy of our attention to support training opportunities that originated from various other groups in the wider European community of science. We could, therefore, co-sponsor events organized across Europe, events that are traditional and with well-established prestige, like the NIC school, La Rabida or the Carpathian schools, but also ad-hoc events that were organized due to the perceived need of specific topics, like the R-matrix, FAST26, Cluster 2026 and the CERN instrumentation school. Funds provided to those events were used to expand the attendance of students and young scientists to events otherwise not accessible to them. 


In conclusion, from the activities of task 5.4 and from the discussions of the community at the General Assemblies where we reported, we dare to say that {\bf we recommend highly for future European projects to include explicitly and allocate appropriate funds for the training} of the members or future members of our community. In addition to funds to use in common the European research infrastructure, {\bf we need to care early and consistently for the formation, development and education of the human resources} that remains the basis for our present and future. The experience of the four years of Task 5.4 of EURO-LABS shows that the cost for this is small in comparison to the results.
