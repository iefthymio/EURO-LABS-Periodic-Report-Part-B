\subsection{Access to Research Infrastructures}

\tsubsubsection{Trans-national access (TA) activities}

\subparagraph{Description of the publicity concerning the new opportunities for access} \mbox{}

The Transnational Access (TA) opportunities offered by EURO-LABS across all facilities are advertised on the official EURO-LABS website, providing potential users with comprehensive information on the application process. Furthermore, under Subtask WP2.5.1 (Streamlined Access), the beam facilities available for nuclear physics research within the WP2 package are presented in a harmonized and detailed manner via the dedicated webpage: \url{https://www.slcj.uw.edu.pl/en/tna-euro-labs/}. In collaboration with WP5, this webpage has been enriched with instructive video clips for all facilities, offering users a clear overview of the installations, key technical aspects, and operational capabilities.

Additional dissemination and publicity measures for the TA opportunities include:
i) Targeted advertisements on the primary webpages of all partner facilities;
ii) Promotion of EURO-LABS TAs at international conferences and workshops during presentations and poster sessions delivered by facility staff;
iii) Requirements for supported users to acknowledge and promote the project in their slides and posters when participating in international conferences or workshops. This goes beyond their formal obligation to acknowledge the project in peer-reviewed publications (the complete list of P3 publications is provided in Part A).

Detailed descriptions of specific publicity actions undertaken by selected facilities are provided below.

\textbf{GANIL}: The EURO-LABS transnational access opportunities and regulations are  on the GANIL website \url{https://www.ganil-spiral2.eu/scientists/running-an-experiment-in-ganil/tna-ensar-support/}. The support is also advised to the P.I. of each experimental program during the preparatory phase by the GANIL beam coordinator.

\textbf{GSI-FAIR}: Access opportunities at GSI/FAIR are publicised through several complementary channels to ensure broad visibility within the international user community. Calls for proposals, submission deadlines, and information on available beamtime and experimental facilities are announced on the GSI/FAIR website and distributed via dedicated mailing lists by the Scientific Managing Directors, the User Affairs Office, and the Beamtime Coordinator. The calls are communicated to the major FAIR and GSI experimental collaborations and further disseminated through the research department heads to their national and international partner institutions. In addition, the relevant scientific and technical advisory committees are informed, ensuring that access opportunities reach the widest possible community of potential users. This multi-channel dissemination strategy ensures transparent and timely communication of access opportunities and supports broad participation of the international scientific community.

\textbf{ISOLDE}: Several measures taken to publicize the opportunities offered by EURO-LABS. A dedicated web site: \url{https://isolde.web.cern.ch/index.php/euro-labs-financial-support} os available and includes information and guidelines like: who can apply, how to apply, call for proposals, financial support, application form, open access and open data obligations, structure and services of the research infrastructure. The group leaders of scheduled TA projects are informed via email, and the user community is informed at the annual user meeting. International workshops/conferences are used to inform a wider scientific community and national representatives informed regularly at collaboration meetings and expected to inform their communities. Last the EURO-LABS TA program is advertised in the ISOLDE yearly newsletter.


\textbf{IFIN-HH TANDEM}: Access to the IFIN-HH accelerator complex is decided by a Program Advisory Committee that meets yearly. A call for proposals to the user community is issued before each PAC meeting and is advertised via email lists and our website.

\textbf{INFN-LNL}: Calls for proposals are posted on the LNL (\url{https://www.lnl.infn.it/en/eurolabs-financial-support/}).  The TA information is available, along with information on eligibility conditions, description on how to apply, Open Access and Open Data Obligations and instructions on how to acknowledge the support of EURO-LABS in publications. Annual reports are published yearly to inform the community.


\textbf{NLC-SLCJ}: In the webpage of the facility \url{https://www.slcj.uw.edu.pl/en/euro-labs-transnational-access-to-the-heavy-ion-laboratory/} all the information concerning the access are described. There are two calls for proposals per year.



\textbf{NLC-CCB}:The CCB facility is accessible to users through transnational access based on procedure including submission of the proposal, evaluation of the proposed experiment and decision on granting funding. The information about call for the proposals is sent into the mailing list and can be obtained from the website (see \url{https://experimentsccb.ifj.edu.pl}).



\textbf{ALTO}: The EURO-LABS supported access opportunities are published on the ALTO web site on the same page as the call for proposals. The spokespersons of experimental proposals which have passed the first scientific stage of selection are then reminded via email of the possibility to apply for EUROLABS TNA support for their experiment, with priority given to early career researchers (Ph.D students and postdocs), and new users of the ALTO facility. 

\textbf{JYU}: Calls for proposals are bi-annual and due to the long-standing practice of having fixed submission dates, the deadlines for JYFL-ACCLAB are reasonably well-known within the user community. In addition, the possibilities for access and available service provision can be found on the University and facility web-site and through several national and international portal pages (e.g. research.fi). Calls are also announced through various mailing lists (local user database, ISOLDE and NUSTAR collaboration lists, NuPECC).


\textbf{CLEAR (CNA - ATOMKI - IST)}: The access opportunities were made public by specific calls, made public every 4 months. The calls were publicised in the facilities (CNA, IST, ATOMKI) web pages, in the EURO-LABS web page, and through the major mailing lists at European facilities (ISOLDE, GSI). There is also a specific portal with all the relevant information and an online application form \url{https://institucional.us.es/clear/}.


\textbf{ECT*}: The access to the ECT* theory facility is described on the webpage \url{https://www.ectstar.eu/}. Calls for conference hosting are made also through the website and the extensive mailing list of ECT* associates.

\textbf{WP3 facilities}: The WP3 facilities promoted the TA opportunities offered by EURO-LABS at international conferences and workshops, in particular when presenting their infrastructures to the accelerator community. For example, the project and its access programme were presented at the 17th International Particle Accelerator Conference (IPAC'26) and described in a contribution to its proceedings\footnote{I.~Efthymiopoulos \textit{et al.}, ``EURO-LABS: Integrating European Research Infrastructures for Physical Sciences'', in \textit{Proc.\ IPAC'26}, paper MOP7145, \href{https://doi.org/10.18429/JACoW-IPAC2026-MOP7145}{doi:10.18429/JACoW-IPAC2026-MOP7145}.}.

Furthermore, the TA offer funded by EURO-LABS was advertised on the websites of the facilities participating in WP3, for example on the KIT web pages \url{https://www.ibpt.kit.edu/alfa.php}, among the films (partially) funded by EURO-LABS, and \url{https://www.ibpt.kit.edu/user.php}, under the four points of the `Proposal Submission System'.


\subparagraph{Description of the selection procedure}\mbox{}


The selection structures and procedures across all WPs were established in alignment with the descriptions provided in the DoA. These structures and procedures varied significantly based on the type, scope, and size of the research facilities, coupled with local constraints related to beam authorization and planning at each facility. Detailed information for each WP is provided below:



\textbf{WP2}: The RIs in WP2 featured distinct USPs (cf. Table~\ref{tab:usp-wp2}), whose composition aligned with the provisions set out in the GA. As a general rule across all facilities, all approved experiments fulfilling the TA eligibility criteria received funding. The funding level was generally proportional to the number of access hours allocated to the respective research group by the Program Advisory Committee (PAC) or the International Advisory Committee (IAC). However, priority was given to new users, early-career researchers, and those with limited resources relative to established researchers, ensuring they could secure the necessary support to pursue their research programs at EURO-LABS RIs. While all projects requesting funding received financial support, budgetary constraints meant that not all individual participants could be funded. The frequency of USP meetings (cf. Table~\ref{tab:usp-wp2-meet}) ranged from one to three times per year, depending on the schedule of the Calls for Proposals and the timeline of the scheduled experiments. These meetings were conducted in person, via Zoom, or in a hybrid format. When necessary, supplementary decisions were made via email exchange (see Table~\ref{tab:usp-wp2-meet} for details).


\textbf{WP3}: A User Selection Panel (USP) is set up for each Task as described in the GA. The USPs were established in P1 (see Table~\ref{tab:usp-wp3}) and continued to operate throughout P3. The frequency of the USP meetings (see Table~\ref{tab:usp-wp3-meet}) varied, ranging from a few per year, depending on the volume of TA requests from the facilities, and moderated by the Task Leaders.


\textbf{WP4}: \mbox{}
A common USP has been established for all facilities of WP4. The USP evaluated all WP4 proposals following a prior check by the Facility Coordinator, and, in some cases (CERN, DESY, PSI) approval by the laboratory Scientific Committee. The USP approvals were carried out by email. All submitted applications in P3 were approved.

\tsubsubsection{Virtual access (VA) activities}



A new VA infrastructure within the EURO-LABS project, \textbf{Theo4Exp}, includes three installations: \textbf{MeanField4Exp} (hosted by IFJ PAN, Krakow), \textbf{Reaction4Exp} (hosted by the University of Sevilla) and \textbf{Structure4Exp} (hosted by the University of Milano). The infrastructure is open to users worldwide since February 2024, at first with part of the envisaged programs and then progressively incorporating the remaining programs until the planned scope was fully completed. An International Review Panel (IRP)  has met every year to review and validate progress. The EU funding of personnel to carry out this VA platform has created increased collaboration between theorists and experimentalists. Codes have now been made accessible to every scientist, and the associated results can be easily compared. This provides an easier and more effective tool to design experiments than possible before.

The VA infrastructure has been available since 1st February 2024 at \href{https://institutional.us.es/theo4exp}. The link to each installation – MeanField4Exp, Reaction4Exp and Structure4Exp – is accessible from this webpage. The infrastructure started operating with some of the codes and was progressively expanded until the incorporation of all the planned codes (more precisely, the different types of calculations) that was completed in 2025.


In the 3rd Reporting Period 3953 AU (compared to the almost 6000 over the whole project duration), counted as each started hour of active use of the virtual service, were provided to 357 users (533 over the whole projects). The following virtual access services were finalized or updated in P3 in the 3 installations:

In IFJ-PAN \textbf{MeanField4Exp}: 

The service MeanField4Exp (\url{https://meanfield4exp.ifj.edu.pl}) combines a series of applications representing branches of nuclear mean-field theory. The present realization evolved from the so-called Realistic Universal Phenomenological Mean Field approach using a deformed Woods-Saxon Hamiltonian with its universal parametrization, where typically 9 parameters are optimized to address about 3000 nuclei. Numerous advanced computer codes are based on combinations of methods from nuclear structure theory and quantum mechanics with advanced techniques from applied mathematics, such as inverse-problem theory and Monte Carlo simulations for optimizing Hamiltonian parameters, graph theory for analysing nuclear shape transitions in multi-dimensional deformation spaces, and group theory for addressing nuclear symmetries.

Several advanced nuclear structure theory computer programs developed earlier were adapted to the processors available at IFJ-PAN. In parallel, the interactive software allowing communication between external logged-in users and our VA infrastructure was tested. The whole system allows interactive communication with the computer programs as well as access to pre-calculated data sets according to user demand, with numerical results transferred in a professional output format (usually diagrams ready for publication). External users can select among the following options:

1. Single-nucleon energies as functions of nuclear shapes: 
The user can generate the single-nucleon energy diagrams after specifying the proton and neutron numbers of the nucleus needed. One needs to select the nuclear multipole mean-field deformation types and ranges, and the nucleon level labelling according to proposed standards used typically in the literature.

2. 1D multi-nucleus representation of nuclear energies according to the Macroscopic-Microscopic Method:
This option allows generating comparative 1D nuclear energy diagrams employing the phenomenological Macroscopic-Microscopic Method for a chain of isotopes and/or isotones. Our algorithms employ the Realistic Phenomenological Nuclear Mean-Field Theory Approach with the Universal Woods-Saxon Hamiltonian. The user needs to select a central nucleus by entering the proton Z and neutron N numbers and select the range of nuclei by adjusting the intervals of Z and N. Selected nucleon numbers become the labels of the plotted curves. As usual in the present system of programs the user selects a parameter set for the Woods-Saxon Hamiltonian from the drop-down menu.

3. Shape evolution with spin according to macroscopic models:
The user of this option is invited to select the proton and neutron numbers of the nucleus needed from the pre-calculated database of results for even-even nuclei only. Potential energy surfaces were pre-calculated using multiprocessor computer systems within an ensemble of five-dimensional original deformation spaces. The graphical representation corresponds to choosing two deformation variables as x- and y-coordinates.

4. Single-nucleon Routhians as functions of the rotational frequency:
This option allows generating the single particle Routhians (single-nucleon energies in the rotating coordinate frame) calculated using the 3D-cranking approach.

5. Nuclear shapes and 3D nuclear surfaces
This option can be seen as providing pedagogical representations of the nuclear shapes and, in particular, shape symmetries. The user can model nuclear surfaces based on specified deformation parameters.

6. Effective GDR strength functions for given spin and temperature
This option allows the user to model the giant dipole resonance (GDR) strength function in terms of the evolution with nuclear angular momentum and temperature according to macroscopic nuclear models. The user may choose the nucleus and the model parameters after consulting the provided elementary references. Graphical aspects are controlled with self-explanatory input data. The user has a parameter choice limited by a pre-calculated database containing results for even-even nuclei only.

7. 3D representations of single-nucleon probability density functions
This option provides illustrations of the spatial behaviour of the nucleonic wave functions.



In USE Sevilla \textbf{Reaction4Exp}: 

The service calculates various types of scattering of interest to contemporary experiment. To perform these calculations, the codes used are the Equivalent-Photon Model (EPM), the FRESCO code, the REGINA code, and a classical-scattering program. The main page of Reaction4Exp (\url{https://reaction4exp.us.es) allows the following options}:


1. Coulomb breakup (Equivalent-Photon Model): 
The EPM code calculates differential Coulomb breakup cross sections from external transition probabilities, as functions of both angle and energy. Multipolarities included are dipole and quadrupole for electric transitions and only dipole for magnetic transitions. The results are presented in text format and graphically displayed. As output, the code provides: the angular distribution of inelastic/breakup cross section, the energy distribution of inelastic/breakup cross section, and the inelastic/breakup probability distribution.



2. Elastic scattering (Optical and classical models):
The elastic scattering calculation can be performed with either the Optical Model (OM) or a classical model. The OM provides the elastic cross section of the reaction considered, at a given energy in the laboratory frame, when an optical potential is provided to describe the interaction between projectile and target. The code used is FRESCO. The results are presented in text format and graphically displayed. As an output, it provides: the angular distribution for the elastic cross section, the absorption and total reaction cross section as a function of the total angular momentum, and the modulus of the elastic S-matrix as a function of the total angular momentum. The classical model provides results that include deflection functions, trajectories, and turning points.



3. Inelastic scattering (coupled channels and distorted-wave Born approximation)"
The inelastic calculation predicts scattering to excited states. It uses a coupled-channel (CC) formalism using a rotational model for the excited states of the projectile or the target. It is possible to perform the calculation in first-order perturbation theory, that is, in distorted-wave Born approximation (DWBA). As output, the code provides reaction cross sections, the elastic angular distribution, and inelastic angular distributions to the different excited states. Additionally, it includes the integrated cross section for all states. The code used for this is again FRESCO. The potential generator is also included in this type of calculation.



4. Transfer reactions:
The transfer calculation provides the cross-section distributions for reactions in which one particle from the projectile is transferred to the target (or vice versa) using the DWBA formalism, both in prior and post form. The installation uses FRESCO and provides the options for the initial (projectile and target) and final (ejectile and residual nucleus) partitions. It is necessary to include a couple of binding potentials together with several optical potentials. All these ingredients are clearly specified for the users. As output, the code provides the angular distribution for elastic and transfer cross sections, the reaction cross section, and a table with the integrated transfer cross section to the states considered in the final partition.

5. Folding potentials (São Paulo Potential and Brazilian Nuclear Potential):
The REGINA code calculates the São Paulo Potential version 2 (SPP2) and the Brazilian Nuclear Potential (BNP), using nuclear densities and distributions for a large variety of nuclei, including theoretical and experimental densities. These folding potentials can later be used in elastic, inelastic and transfer calculations within this installation or in other programs outside the platform. The output provides the folding potential and the elastic cross-section angular distribution obtained with that potential (taking the imaginary as a parameter times the real part).


In UMIL \textbf{Structure4Exp}: Three computer codes have been made available. A brief general explanation of the overall purpose of the installation and the available computer codes is presented in the main page of the Structure4Exp installation (https://ns4exp.mi.infn.it). The user can specify the code of interest and access pages with more detailed explanations. In particular, the user is guided to prepare proper inputs through a user-friendly interface. The limits of the three codes are clearly spelled out. Shell-model calculations can place significant demands on CPU and RAM resources. Therefore, a preliminary estimation is conducted to assess the computing resources necessary to complete the calculation specified in the user input, aiming to prevent the submission of overly demanding computations. The webpages have been extensively tested, and comprehensive accounting information is available concerning the number of users and their CPU usage.
The services will enable experimentalists to check by themselves the predictions for observables of general interest in nuclear physics. The three codes allow comparison between different theories:



1. HF-RPA:
This is a software tool designed to perform self-consistent calculations of the ground-state proper-ties of spherical atomic nuclei, as well as their (vibrational) excitation spectra, including multipole strength functions and other related properties. The strength functions are associated with the typical isoscalar, isovector, and electromagnetic operators that are widely employed in the literature. The program is based on the HF-RPA theoretical framework and makes use of Skyrme-type interactions and energy functionals. The code is limited to even-even, spherical nuclei, neglecting pairing correlations and operating within closed shells or sub-shells. Certain widely used Skyrme sets are built in the code and can be chosen from a simple menu, but the user has the possibility to input the parameters of a different interaction.
  
2. HFBCS-QRPA:
This tool has a structure similar to the HF-RPA software. It includes the possibility to account for pairing correlations in ground and excited states, calculated in BCS and QRPA, respectively. In this way, the properties of collective states of open-shell nuclei can be assessed. As compared with the previous HF-RPA case, the input also requires the specification of the strength of a zero-range pairing interaction, together with an associated energy cut-off.

Both HF-RPA and HFBCS-QRPA are useful tools for experimentalists who aim at studying giant resonances in spherical nuclei, but also low-lying multipole excitations like pygmy states. Charge radii, neutron skins, dipole polarizabilities, and other observables of interest for an experimental proposal or for other purposes can be accessed.

3. KSHELL:
The KSHELL code enables users to perform realistic nuclear shell-model calculations, using two-body interactions within the M-scheme basis representation. The diagonalization procedure of the Hamiltonian matrix is based on a variant of the Lanczos method called the thick-restart block Lanczos method. Depending on the model space, shell-model calculations may require many more computing resources, in terms of CPU and RAM, than the (Q)RPA computations of the other codes. The KSHELL code is capable to handle massive parallel calculations on supercomputers, but the limited capabilities of our server put rather severe constraints on the calculations that can be carried out using our service. Accordingly, for a given input proposed by the user, a preliminary load estimate is made and, if the calculation requires the diagonalization of matrices that are larger than 106, then the user is warned that the desired calculation is not feasible.


Concluding,  in P3 the VA facility, \textbf{Theo4Exp}, has established three platforms (MeanFieldExp, Reaction4Exp, Structure4Exp) that provide results from various nuclear models to quantities of experimental interest, in order to facilitate the interpretation of already performed experiments and the design of new experiments.  The platforms  became fully operational and integrated. The predictions from structure and reaction calculations are now at the fingertips of experimentalists anywhere. The infrastructure has been widely promoted and currently serves more than 500 registered users. These platforms represent an important achievement of EURO-LABS, improving access to theoretical support and strengthening collaboration between theorists and experimentalists. They are a long-lasting tribute to the EU investment on science, enabling progress in fundamental and applied physics for year to come.
