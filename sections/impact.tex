\subsection{Impact}
\label{sec:impact}

%\todo{Include in this section whether the information on section 2.1 of the DoA  (how your project will contribute to the expected impacts) is still relevant or needs to be updated. Include further details in the latter case.}


In P3, EURO-LABS made substantial contributions through the provision of TA support to the project's leading Research Infrastructures (RIs). This support was pivotal for frontline experiments, research and development (R\&D), training young researchers, and advancing Open Science initiatives.

Within \textbf{WP2}, the executed Transnational Access (TA) projects addressed high-priority topics in fundamental nuclear physics and its applications, strictly aligning with the recommendations of the NUPECC Long Range Plan. The resulting outcomes will be disseminated through peer-reviewed scientific journals and conference proceedings. Furthermore, the service improvement activities undertaken in P3 have yielded a lasting impact on the operational quality across several Research Infrastructures (RIs). Key advancements include the deployment of a centralized webpage detailing TA facilities and project proposal procedures, alongside the implementation of novel tools designed for optimized remote access.

The facilities involved in \textbf{WP3} are unique infrastructures for accelerator technology R\&D, and the TA projects carried out in P3 demonstrate an impact that extends well beyond high-energy physics. At INCT/RAPID, electron-beam irradiation experiments addressed concrete societal applications, ranging from the safety of food and livestock feed and the resilience of crops to polymer materials for medicine and sustainable packaging, and the conversion of greenhouse gases into syngas. CERN-CLEAR continued to support R\&D on FLASH radiotherapy, a promising approach to cancer treatment based on ultra-high dose rates, while at KIT-KARA beam studies contributed directly to the design of the future FCC-ee collider, including beam-based alignment, beam stability and electron-cloud mitigation. At CERN-HiRadMat, a series of novel experiments investigated the interaction of high-intensity beams with materials, of relevance both to future accelerator components and to laboratory astrophysics. 

Finally, \textbf{WP3} TA projects advanced R\&D on superconducting cavity materials and on particle detectors. Next-generation SRF cavities rely on high-$T_c$ superconducting films such as NbTiN and Nb$_3$Sn, which can operate at \SI{4.2}{K} with the performance of bulk Nb at \SI{2}{K} and thus significantly reduce cryogenic costs. At CEA-IRFU/Synergium, the MACHAFILM platform prepared Nb cavities and quadrupole resonators from HZB, STFC and other laboratories with surface treatments that established reliable RF baselines for future thin-film depositions, and used tunnelling spectroscopy, a strong predictor of RF performance, to characterise NbTiN and Nb$_3$Sn films from FNAL, CERN, JLab, STFC and INFN. It also tested Nb cavities treated with atomic layer deposition and annealing, a new approach pioneered by CEA to mitigate two-level-system defects, which is also relevant to the use of SRF cavities in quantum technologies. INFN-LASA complemented these activities with the first experimental tests of plasma processing on a PIP-II LB650 cavity, while INFN-LNF/BTF supported the development and testing of novel particle detectors for future colliders.

The majority of the executed TA projects of \textbf{WP4} are of vital importance for the successful completion of the HL-LHC project, the focal point of the European Strategy for Particle Physics (ESPP) in 2013 and its updates in 2020 and 2026. The remaining ones are well synchronized with the stipulations of the ECFA Detector R\&D Roadmap, serving either FCC-ee as CERN's next flagship project, or the more distant endeavour towards FCC-hh, the highest energy hadron collider. WP4 backs up the newly set up DRD collaborations, by providing them with access to top-level infrastructure needed for their detector R\&D. The stipulations of the ESPP update, adopted by CERN Council in May 2026, will require a stronger focus on detector R\&D, directed towards FCC-ee. Although these developments exceed the EURO-LABS project duration, WP4 has set up the fundaments by fostering the development of novel detector concepts like a variety of MAPS and LGAD based sensors.


The overall project delivered the services, essential for detector R\&D. The resulting scientific impact is two-fold: scientific results in instrumentation are being published in scientific journals and conference proceedings during the duration of the project. The more glorious part with the physics results of the HL-LHC experiments and their follow-ups at future colliders will, however, start emerging during the next decade and beyond, when the detectors being tested at WP4 RIs will be put to their proper usage and deliver precious physics data.

community.
The Authentication and Authorization Infrastructure (AAI), providing secure user authentication through integration with established identity providers, such as eduGAIN and ORCID, was successfully released according to schedule. With regard to open data, the first functional version of the OpenNP catalogue, where avalaible nuclear physics experimental data are listed,  has been successfully released and new data platforms have been created. All these activities will continue beyond the end of EURO-LABS, continuing to fostering the adoption of FAIR practices at the various RIs. 

Within Task 5.3, 
the new GeOFF (Generic Optimization Framework and Front-End) toolkit, based on Machine Learning techniques, was deployed at GSI and CERN, where it has been successfully used for beam optimization. Work on adapting GeOFF to a laser-driven particle accelerator at CEA (France) is ongoing. 
The development of these new techniques, that can be applied across multiple RIs, has a significant impact on 
enhancing scientific output in general.

Within Task 5.4, training activities have continued to progress. Two Basic Training Schools (BTS) and two Advanced Training Schools on Accelerator Operation were held in spring 2025 and spring 2026. In addition, a further training school dedicated to Open Data was organized in winter 2026. 
Training young researchers, as we did in EURO-LABS, has a crucial impact on strengthening the global scientific
impact of European research teams and ensuring the future of nuclear and particle physics research.
